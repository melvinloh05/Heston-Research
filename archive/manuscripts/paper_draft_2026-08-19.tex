\begin{abstract}
Learned option pricers return Greeks by automatic differentiation, and trading
desks hedge with those derivatives, yet standard training controls only the price
in $L^{2}$ and leaves the derivative fields uncontrolled. Theory establishes that
the discrete-rebalancing hedging error of a surrogate is governed at leading order
by its gamma, and the trained pricers that supervise gamma evaluate hedging under
the same process used for training and charge no transaction costs. This paper
reports the intervening experiment, pre-registered in full before any training run.
A parametric Heston physics-informed network is trained over a Feller-constrained
parameter hypercube, and delta-only hedging profit and loss is measured under
perturbed Heston, Bates, and Merton dynamics with proportional costs charged. A
supervision ladder attributes the effect by order, a gamma-label-noise
dose-response separates label correctness from generic smoothing, and a
pre-registered two-by-two design with an excess-turnover statistic adjudicates the
mechanism. Qualitatively, derivative supervision delivers large and cleanly
attributed gains in held-out Greek accuracy that price-only training does not reach
at any information budget, while the pre-registered hedging claim fails: the
benefit that exists is carried by first-order supervision, is already present under
well-specified dynamics, and is exhausted by transaction costs. Quantitatively,
out-of-distribution gamma and vega relative RMSE fall by $86$ to $93\%$ against the
residual-only baseline, of which a $60\%$ gamma reduction is attributable to gamma
labels specifically; the confirmatory CVaR$_{95}$ contrast returns $+0.02\%$
against a pre-registered $10\%$ bar (10 seeds, pooled $95\%$ CI $[-0.024, +0.024]$
in loss units), and the add-gamma rung is a significant reversal of $+0.057$
$[+0.053, +0.062]$. At zero cost the same contrast is $+31.5\%$, of which $28.9$
of $31.5$ percentage points come from delta supervision and $27.2$ are present
in-model.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Once a learned pricer produces a value, automatic differentiation delivers $\Delta$,
$\Gamma$, and vega in the same forward pass, so the derivatives of the learned
surface are traded quantities rather than diagnostics. Standard training
nevertheless controls only the price in $L^{2}$, permitting a network to fit prices
closely while the slope of its hedge ratio remains noisy or biased. Supervising
derivatives is established practice: differential machine learning trains on values
together with pathwise algorithmic-adjoint differentials
\citep{huge2020differential}, the financial instance of Sobolev training on
derivative norms \citep{czarnecki2017sobolev}. A second fact compounds the first.
The pricer is always trained under a model that is wrong, since the world is not
Heston \citep{heston1993closed}, so the field a desk trades is a wrong-model Greek
evaluated on paths the model did not generate.

Both ends of the literature name the resulting gap without closing it. On the
theory side, \citet{noguer2026derivative} proves that the discrete-rebalancing
hedging error of a surrogate is governed at leading order by its gamma
(Thms.~2--3, Prop.~3) and shows that first-order supervision does not reliably
control a held-out second-order Greek; that paper contains no hedging experiment,
and its stochastic-volatility experiments use a fixed-random-feature ridge
surrogate rather than a trained pricer. On the practice side, DeepSVM
\citep{chakhoyan2025deepsvm} reaches the same diagnosis from the residual end: a
physics-informed operator network attains low residual loss on the parametric
Heston problem yet reports oscillatory deltas and locally negative gammas near the
money, finds that a soft gamma-positivity penalty neither stabilised the learned
Greeks nor preserved pricing accuracy, and identifies explicit Sobolev
regularisation as a necessary direction. The nearest trained configuration,
\citet{sakuma2026a}, supervises $\Delta$, $\Gamma$, and vega alongside a PIDE
residual, but evaluates hedging under the same well-specified process used for
training and charges no transaction costs, obtaining nearly indistinguishable
profit-and-loss distributions across arms. Two mechanisms are live and left
unseparated. Under the model-uncertainty channel, accurate curvature copes with the
gap between assumed and realised dynamics, the account \citet{armstrong2024deep}
give at the level of strategy choice while holding Greeks analytic. Under the
transaction-cost channel, accurate gamma yields smoother, lower-turnover delta
paths. Whether second-order supervision improves the hedging decision under
misspecification, and through which mechanism, has not been tested.

This paper reports that test. A parametric Heston physics-informed network with
inputs $(S, K, \tau, \kappa, \theta, \xi, \rho, v_0)$ is trained over a
Feller-constrained hypercube so that held-out-parameter claims are coherent, and
delta-only hedging is evaluated when the pricer is trained under one
data-generating process and deployed under another. The primary evidence is
held-out hedging profit and loss with costs charged rather than Greek RMSE, which
cannot answer the question: in-domain it measures fit to the same labels used for
training, in-model it is invisible to hedging outcomes, and cross-model it is
dominated by a model-bias term identical across arms. The entire acceptance block,
including the direction-free mechanism falsifier, was frozen in
\texttt{heston\_benchmark\_v6.yaml} before any training run, and an oracle-headroom
diagnostic established before training that the design could resolve a gamma error
of the baseline's measured size and could not resolve one of the supervised arm's
size. All reported contrasts carry both the registered pooled path-level bootstrap
statistic and its per-seed companion, and the two are never conflated.

The pre-registered confirmatory test failed, and the failure is reported first and
unhedged. The contributions are consequently as follows. (i) A causal design for
Greek supervision, comprising a supervision ladder from price to ${+}\Delta$ to
${+}\Delta{+}\Gamma$ to ${+}\Delta{+}\Gamma{+}\nu$ under otherwise identical arms, a
residual\,$\times$\,supervision factorial, a gamma-label-noise dose-response, and a
pre-registered mechanism adjudication with both outcomes and the null committed as
publishable in advance. (ii) A held-out Greek-accuracy result with clean ladder
attribution: out-of-distribution $\Gamma$ and $\nu$ relative RMSE fall by $86$ to
$93\%$, the $\Gamma$ component is attributable to gamma labels, and an
information-matched price-only baseline plateaus far above it, so the gain is not
reachable by spending more price data. (iii) A pre-registered null on the hedging
side, with the add-gamma rung a significant reversal at the registered cost tier,
together with the evidence that separates its causes: the residual-only baseline
under-trades relative to the oracle by $38\%$, supervision restores oracle-level
turnover and pays for it once costs are charged, and the effect that supervision
does deliver is present without misspecification. (iv) Two design findings, namely
that the registered mechanism menu was incomplete and that the design's own
in-model consistency cell detected the omission, and that a Black--Scholes gamma
label produces a better delta-only hedger than exact Greeks, which a
minimum-variance-delta comparator explains.

\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Learned surface, supervision objective, and arms}
\label{sec:loss}
Under the risk-neutral Heston model \citep{heston1993closed}, the European-call
price $C(S,v,\tau)$ in time-to-maturity $\tau$ solves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hestonpde}
\partial_\tau C=\tfrac12 vS^{2}\partial_{SS}C+\rho\xi vS\,\partial_{Sv}C
+\tfrac12\xi^{2}v\,\partial_{vv}C+(r-q)S\,\partial_{S}C
+\kappa(\theta-v)\,\partial_{v}C-rC,
\end{equation}
with $C(S,v,0)=(S-K)^{+}$, where the variance follows a square-root process of
mean-reversion rate $\kappa$, level $\theta$, and volatility of volatility $\xi$,
correlated with the spot at $\rho$. One parametric surface
$C_\phi(S,K,\tau;\vartheta)$, $\vartheta=(\kappa,\theta,\xi,\rho,v_0)$, is trained
over all eight inputs, the initial-variance input $v_0$ serving as the variance
coordinate. Greeks are obtained by automatic differentiation:
$\Delta=\partial_S C_\phi$, $\Gamma=\partial_{SS}C_\phi$, the variance vega
$\nu=\partial_{v_0}C_\phi$, and vanna $\partial^2_{S v_0}C_\phi$, the last
supervised only in an optional arm and otherwise a diagnostic. The squared residual
of \eqref{eq:hestonpde} on collocation points defines
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{pde}}$ \citep{raissi2019physics}. Every arm is a flag setting
of one loss,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:loss}
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{price}}
+\lambda_{\mathrm{pde}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{pde}}
+\lambda_{\mathrm{bc}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{bc}}
+\lambda_{D}\mathcal{L}_{D}+\lambda_{G}\mathcal{L}_{G}
+\lambda_{\nu}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}
\;\bigl[+\lambda_{\mathrm{vanna}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{vanna}}\bigr],
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{L}_{D},\mathcal{L}_{G},\mathcal{L}_{\nu}$ are scale-normalised
mean-squared errors of $\partial_S C_\phi$, $\partial_{SS}C_\phi$,
$\partial_{v_0}C_\phi$ against oracle labels. The gamma normalisation is frozen from
the true consensus so that every dose-response arm shares one scale. Weights are
selected on validation data only, with the test set locked in code. The unsupervised
baseline is the standard Heston PINN of \citet{hainaut2024option}. Terminal-condition
treatment is held identical across all arms, since hard-constrained ansatz
constructions \citep{lagaris1998artificial} embed the payoff kink into every
derivative field at all maturities and any asymmetry there would confound the
supervision effect. Table~\ref{tab:arms} lists the arms.

\subsection{Oracle, training distribution, and evaluation splits}
\label{sec:oracle}
Labels and hedging references derive from the trap-free characteristic-function
branch of \citet{albrecher2007little} with Fourier pricing per
\citet{carrmadan1999option}, differentiated before discretisation rather than after,
and cross-validated against high-order finite differences on the price grid, a
Monte-Carlo leg, and a Craig--Sneyd ADI leg on Feller-stressed points. Legs (i) and
(ii) share the characteristic-function machinery and are not independent witnesses.
Points where legs disagree above tolerance are masked under a rule declared
pre-training and subjected to three neutrality checks; the panels, including two
marginals that worsened after the ADI leg was corrected, are in
Appendix~\ref{app:oracle}. Table~\ref{tab:config} collects the settings.

Per-regime training would make the held-out-parameter Greek leg incoherent, since
the three named Heston regimes share $\kappa=2.0$ and $\rho=-0.50$ exactly, so a
network trained only on them receives no gradient signal in those directions.
Held-out evaluation therefore has two tiers: \texttt{near\_feller} is interpolation
with its ball excised, and \texttt{strong\_neg\_corr} at $\rho=-0.90$ lies beyond
the sampled range, the realised minimum being $\rho=-0.7989$, so the extrapolation
is literal. Both tiers receive Greek-accuracy and hedging metrics; cross-model
transfer is hedging-only.

\subsection{Hedging backtest, mechanism adjudication, and thresholds}
\label{sec:hedge}
The strategy is pure delta hedging of the underlying, held fixed across arms so that
Greek estimation quality is the only varying factor; an option leg would let
instrument-level costs dominate and confound the mechanism
\citep{armstrong2024deep}. Profit and loss is self-financing, every arm pays the same
oracle premium at the training parameters, the terminal liability is marked at the
true-DGP price at $T'$, and with $L=-\mathrm{PnL}$ the reported statistic is
$\mathrm{CVaR}_{95}$, the mean of the worst $5\%$. Three competitors are run: a
Monte-Carlo-Greek hedger; the oracle-$\Delta$ hedger as the ceiling defining the
fraction of the gap closed; and a smoothed baseline applying a pure no-trade band to
the standard PINN's delta, the classical proportional-cost policy class
\citep{whalley1997asymptotic}, with band width chosen on validation cells. The
mechanism falsifier is excess turnover over the oracle on identical paths,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tex}
T_{\mathrm{ex}}
=\sum_i\bigl|\Delta\hat{\Delta}_i\bigr|
-\sum_i\bigl|\Delta\Delta^{\circ}_i\bigr|,
\end{equation}
so that $T_{\mathrm{ex}}<0$ denotes an arm that trades less than the oracle.
Channel adjudication uses a pre-registered $2\times2$ crossing
$\{\theta_{\mathrm{test}}=\theta_{\mathrm{train}},\,\neq\}$ with
$\{\kappa_{\mathrm{tc}}=0,\,>0\}$: a gap already present at zero cost indicates the
robustness channel; a gap near zero at zero cost that widens with cost while
$T_{\mathrm{ex}}$ falls indicates the cost channel; an unmoved $T_{\mathrm{ex}}$
rejects the cost channel whatever the profit and loss shows; neither anywhere is the
pre-registered null. The in-model, zero-cost corner must reproduce the no-gap result
of \citet{sakuma2026a} within $2\%$ as a consistency check that is not pass or fail.

Verdicts distinguish three states, recorded separately in every table and figure. A
pass or a fail is a contrast evaluated against its threshold; a \emph{null} means the
artifact was legitimately absent and the evaluation was not attempted; an
\emph{error} means the evaluation was attempted and failed. Merging the last two
would let a broken run read as an absence of effect. Every contrast reports two
uncertainties, which answer different questions and are never substituted for one
another: the registered pooled stratified paired bootstrap over the shared
common-random-number path banks, which asks whether the trained hedgers differ on
this path population, and the per-seed companion interval, which asks whether the
treatment replicates across training runs. Language reserved for generalisation
requires the second. The acceptance block, reproduced verbatim in
Appendix~\ref{app:thresholds} with its dated amendment record, requires a
$\geq10\%$ relative $\mathrm{CVaR}_{95}$ improvement with a paired-bootstrap
interval excluding zero at the confirmatory cell; rung~2 to beat rung~1 at that
cell; demonstrated monotonicity in the dose-response; and out-of-distribution
$\Gamma$ and $\nu$ RMSE each to fall by $\geq15\%$ at price parity within $10\%$.

Before any arm trained, an oracle-headroom diagnostic hedged the confirmatory
instrument with oracle Greeks and under an anisotropic, spot-dominated corruption
field alongside an amplitude-matched independent comparator, requiring every seed's
paired interval to exclude zero. Because the delta clip amplifies the delivered
gamma error across the decision band, by factors of $1.00$, $1.21$, and $1.41$ at
nominal $\sigma_{\mathrm{rel}}\in\{0.05,0.10,0.15\}$, pilots are compared against
the effective rather than the nominal gamma sigma. At the residual-only baseline's
own measured gamma error ($\sigma_{\mathrm{rel}}=0.0929$, effective
$\sigma_\Gamma=4.51\times10^{-3}$, clipped fraction $0.063$, inside the region of
validity) the spread was $+100.8\%$, $+37.5\%$, and $+16.0\%$ at the three
registered cost tiers, clearing the $10\%$ bar in every seed. At the supervised
arm's measured gamma error ($\sigma_\Gamma=1.25\times10^{-4}$) no tier cleared, the
spread being $+2.5\%$, $+0.44\%$, and $+0.08\%$. The design therefore had headroom
to detect an error of the baseline's size and none to detect an error of the treated
arm's size, which is the correct frame for the hedging results below.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Pre-registered outcomes}
\label{sec:reg}

Table~\ref{tab:verdicts} reports every registered endpoint against its frozen
threshold. The confirmatory test fails. At the registered cell the relative
$\mathrm{CVaR}_{95}$ improvement of rung~3 over the standard PINN is $+0.02\%$
against a $10\%$ bar, with a pooled interval covering zero and a per-seed interval
covering zero, so the contrast is neither pooled-significant nor seed-robust. The
order-attribution endpoint also fails, and its failure is not a null: the add-gamma
rung is a reversal of $+0.0574$ in loss units, pooled interval $[+0.0527,+0.0621]$
and per-seed interval $[+0.0311,+0.0856]$, both excluding zero on the harmful side.
Its relative size, $-1.2\%$, sits below any economic-relevance floor, so the correct
reading is that gamma supervision is significantly but negligibly harmful at that
cell rather than inert. The registered outcome vocabulary, which admits only
``passes'' and ``failure equals honest null'', cannot express a significant
reversal; that is a defect of the vocabulary and is recorded as such.

Two registered outcomes require their direction stated. The out-of-distribution
Greek endpoint fails on its price-parity conjunct rather than on accuracy:
$\Gamma$ reduction is $0.894$ at \texttt{near\_feller} and $0.864$ at
\texttt{strong\_neg\_corr}, $\nu$ reduction is $0.927$ and $0.930$, against a
registered floor of $0.15$, while the parity term
$\mathrm{relRMSE}_{\mathrm{arm}}/\mathrm{relRMSE}_{\mathrm{base}}-1$ equals $-0.908$
and $-0.821$ and is scored against a two-sided tolerance of $0.10$. The clause as
written rejects an arm for improving the price by nine tenths, and the same clause
admits an arm trained on half-destroyed gamma labels. The registered verdict stands
as a fail and the defect is reported, not repaired after the fact. Second, the
in-model consistency cell was registered against the wrong contrast. The comparison
in \citet{sakuma2026a} is between true deltas and a delta-supervised network, whose
translation here is rung~3 against the oracle in-model; that contrast returns
$+0.35\%$, inside the registered $2\%$ negligibility band, so the null reproduces.
The coded contrast, rung~3 against the residual-only baseline, tests something that
was never run in the source and returns a $27.2\%$ gap. The check flagged, and what
it detected was a fact about the baseline, examined in Section~\ref{sec:mech}.

\subsection{Attribution and Greek accuracy}
\label{sec:attr}

Table~\ref{tab:tiers} gives the hedging profile. At zero cost, supervision closes
$98.9\%$ of the distance from the residual-only baseline to the oracle-$\Delta$
hedger, and the ladder attributes that $31.5\%$ improvement as $28.9$ percentage
points to $+\Delta$, $2.0$ to $+\Gamma$, and $0.5$ to $+\nu$. The effect decays
monotonically as the proportional cost rises, crosses zero near the registered $1\%$
tier, and is a significant harm at $2\%$. The tiers below $1\%$ were added after the
registered verdict was known and are reported as a post-hoc sensitivity analysis
throughout; they change no registered verdict. Their interest is external: the rate
\citet{armstrong2024deep} calibrate for the underlying leg, the only leg a
delta-only hedger trades, is $0.005\%$, at which the effect is $+31.3\%$ and
indistinguishable from frictionless, while their high-cost stress tier of $0.5\%$
gives $+14.5\%$. At and above the registered tier the ranking inverts and the
winner is the no-trade band applied to the worst delta in the study, which at $1\%$
attains $4.591$ against rung~3's $4.859$ and the oracle's $4.899$. The cost regime

\subsection{Mechanism, label correctness, and the minimum-variance comparator}
\label{sec:mech}

The registered rule returns channel (i), because the gap is present at zero cost
with an interval excluding zero, does not widen with cost in the direction of
improvement, and is not accompanied by a reduction in $T_{\mathrm{ex}}$. The
registered \emph{interpretation} of that reading, model-uncertainty robustness, is
not supported by the design's own cells, and the cell that detects this is the
in-model consistency corner the pre-registration required. Of the $31.5\%$ zero-cost
effect under combined misspecification, $27.2\%$ is already present under
well-specified dynamics, so roughly $86\%$ of the effect requires no
misspecification at all. Under increasing misspecification the effect decays rather
than grows: across the Bates severity sweep at zero cost it falls monotonically from
$+27.7\%$ at zero jump intensity to $-4.2\%$ at the most severe cell, and the single
Merton cell gives $+9.8\%$ at zero cost and $-3.7\%$ at $1\%$. Mean profit and loss
is identical across arms at zero cost, at $+0.0773$, so the entire gap is tail
dispersion rather than drift.

The turnover evidence rejects the cost channel and does so with the opposite sign to
the one the falsifier anticipated. The registered falsifier presumed a noisy,
over-trading baseline whose turnover accurate curvature would reduce. The measured
baseline under-trades: its turnover is $0.0248$ against the oracle's $0.0398$, or
$62\%$ of oracle, with $T_{\mathrm{ex}}=-0.696$, because its delta is systematically
damped. Supervision restores oracle-level turnover, rung~3 reaching
$T_{\mathrm{ex}}=-0.007$, and the difference $T_{\mathrm{ex}}(\text{rung
3})-T_{\mathrm{ex}}(\text{rung 0})=+0.689$ has a per-seed interval $[+0.662,+0.715]$
excluding zero on the increasing side. Turnover therefore moved, and it moved
upward, so the cost mechanism operates against the treatment rather than for it. The
operative account consistent with every cell is that supervision improves the
approximation quality of the delta field and thereby reduces discrete-hedge tracking
error, which is a channel outside the registered menu, is the object
\citet{noguer2026derivative} bounds in theory, and is reported here as a design
finding: the menu was incomplete in both of its entries, and the pre-registered
consistency cell detected the omission.

That anomaly has an explanation, and it is the one new phenomenon in the record.
Hedging the same path banks with the Heston minimum-variance delta,
$\Delta_{\mathrm{MV}}=\partial_S C+(\rho\xi/S)\,\partial_v C$, which corrects the
pure spot derivative for the correlated variance move, gives in-model zero-cost
$\mathrm{CVaR}_{95}$ of $1.562$ against the exact-delta oracle's $1.741$, rung~3's
$1.737$, and the Black--Scholes-gamma arm's $1.586$, over five seeds on identical
paths. Exact-delta tracking is therefore measurably suboptimal for discrete
$\mathrm{CVaR}_{95}$ hedging under these dynamics, the minimum-variance correction
recovers the difference, and the biased arm recovers $87\%$ of it. The
Black--Scholes-gamma advantage is thus not evidence that cheap labels suffice for
Greek accuracy, where they plainly do not, but evidence that the registered hedging
metric rewards proximity to the minimum-variance delta rather than proximity to the
exact one. This also retro-explains the dose-response result, and it is reported as
an observed and now partly explained phenomenon rather than as a claim about
optimal hedging in general.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This study asked whether explicit supervision of the derivative of the hedge ratio
improves delta-only hedging under misspecified dynamics beyond first-order
supervision, and through which channel. The pre-registered answer is a failure, and
it is reported as one: at the confirmatory cell the improvement is $+0.02\%$ against
a $10\%$ bar with an interval covering zero, and the rung on which the claim was
placed is a small significant reversal. What survives is different in kind. Sobolev
supervision improves held-out out-of-distribution $\Gamma$ and $\nu$ accuracy by
$86$ to $93\%$, with a $60\%$ $\Gamma$ reduction attributable to gamma labels
specifically and unreachable by information-matched price-only training, while the
hedging benefit that exists, $+31.5\%$ at zero cost with ten seeds and an interval
excluding zero, is carried by delta supervision, is present without
misspecification, decays with transaction costs, and vanishes at the registered
tier. Three findings follow from the design rather than from the treatment: the
registered mechanism menu omitted the tracking channel that the evidence supports
and inverted the sign of the cost channel's premise, and the pre-registered in-model
consistency cell detected both; a no-trade band on the study's worst delta beats
every accurate arm once costs reach $1\%$; and a systematically biased
Black--Scholes gamma label produces a better delta-only hedger than exact Greeks,
which a minimum-variance-delta comparator largely explains.

Five limitations bound these claims. The zero-cost headline is a property of this
baseline's damped delta at this residual weight and this architecture, and
generalises only to baselines with comparable pathology. The registered robustness
row at the alternative residual weight was not run, which is outstanding
registration debt. The dose-response, the residual-free arm, the feedforward arm,
and the minimum-variance comparator carry five seeds rather than ten. No cost-aware
supervised arm was run, so the paper is silent on deployment above the crossing
point. Misspecification is synthetic throughout. The natural next experiments follow
directly: dose the label bias rather than its variance, add a higher-frequency
rebalancing cell in which the discretisation term the theory identifies actually
binds, and pair every accurate arm with a cost-aware policy.

\section{Oracle validation, mask-neutrality panels, and the headroom ladder}
\label{app:oracle}

\paragraph{Fourth-leg diagnosis.} On \texttt{feller\_violating\_volvol}, the ADI
leg's vega disagreed with the characteristic-function leg systematically, exceeding
it on $87.9\%$ of grid points, growing monotonically in maturity from $0.0$ at
$\tau = 0.04$ to $0.0087$ at $\tau = 1.0$, and masking $59.4\%$ of that regime's vega
grid. Four candidate causes were eliminated in order (Table~\ref{tab:adi}). The
survivor was variance-domain truncation: at the previous default the variance domain
is cut inside the mass of the $\xi = 0.60$ regime and the artificial boundary
contaminates $v$-direction derivatives. Raising $v_{\max}$ from $1.6$ to $3.2$
changes the median disagreement from $4.0\mathrm{e}{-}05$ to $2.8\mathrm{e}{-}05$,
so $1.6$ is converged, and \texttt{near\_feller} is flat under the same sweep, which
localises the effect to broad-$\xi$ regimes. The worst relative error on the affected
regime improved from $3.56\mathrm{e}{-}03$ to $1.57\mathrm{e}{-}03$ against a
$5\mathrm{e}{-}03$ bar. What adjudicated the disagreement was narrower than four legs
suggests: the Monte-Carlo band exceeded the disagreement on $85\%$ of points and
could not break the tie. On that regime the vega uncertainty is $1.33$, dominated by
Monte-Carlo noise at $\xi = 0.60$ and binding on $70\%$ of points, which no solver
change lowers.

\paragraph{Mask-neutrality panels.} The Feller-band panel shows the affected band
halving from $0.5192$ to $0.2434$ while every other band is bit-identical, the
control confirming that the change touched only the fourth leg. That band remains
elevated against roughly $0.09$ elsewhere, its excess falling from $5.8\times$ to
$2.7\times$. The marginal-distribution panel moved in both directions: $\kappa$,
$\theta$, and $\xi$ improved ($0.0645 \to 0.0287$, $0.1787 \to 0.1201$,
$0.1284 \to 0.0383$), while $\rho$ and $v_0$ worsened ($0.1242 \to 0.1492$,
$0.0832 \to 0.1266$). The improvement in $\theta$ and $\xi$ is mechanical, since the
Feller ratio is $2\kappa\theta/\xi^{2}$ and band-concentrated masking is itself a
$\theta$ and $\xi$ shift; the deterioration in $\rho$ and $v_0$ follows from the
masked set shrinking by roughly $12\%$ and changing composition. The fix is
therefore not a uniform improvement and is not presented as one. The
curvature-decile panel improved at every decile including the highest
($0.1221 \to 0.1010$), which is the one that matters for gamma supervision, and the
contract's stated failure mode for that panel, the mask rate rising with curvature,
does not occur.

\paragraph{Headroom-gate ladder.} Rungs at $\sigma_{\mathrm{rel}} \in
\{0.05, 0.10, 0.15\}$ are decision rungs; $\{0.20, 0.40\}$ are diagnostic, swept and
plotted identically but excluded from the decision scan (Table~\ref{tab:ladder}).
The bound $\texttt{clipped\_frac\_max} = 0.25$ was not raised to re-admit $0.20$,
whose per-seed maximum straddles it, since a rung whose eligibility depends on which
seeds were drawn is not a decision rung. The bound is structural rather than
statistical: above it the corrupted arm is a saturated hedger pinned at the delta
bound over much of the state space, whose gamma error is the oracle's own $-\Gamma$
rather than the calibrated field, and no trained network's Greek error behaves that
way. Both pilots reported in Section~\ref{sec:hedge} lie inside the region of
validity, at clipped fractions of $0.063$ and $0.000$.
