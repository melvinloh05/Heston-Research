\begin{abstract}
Neural surrogates for parametric differential equations return their derivatives for
free by automatic differentiation, and downstream procedures increasingly consume
those derivatives to make decisions. We pre-registered a test of whether supervising
them improves such a decision, and it failed: at the confirmatory cell the
improvement is $+0.02\%$ against a $10\%$ bar with a $95\%$ interval covering zero,
and the rung carrying the registered causal claim reverses. Every registered
efficacy endpoint failed; the scorecard's consistency check and mechanism
adjudication returned as designed. The same design then identifies why, and the
answer is that the registered scorecard mis-attributes on three axes. Location:
accuracy is scored on a parameter grid while the decision consumes it on a
trajectory, and the two can rank arms oppositely; weighting the error by the
occupancy measure the controller induces predicts realised cost better than either
($\rho = 0.71$ against $0.47$ on the grid, for second derivatives). Direction: a
systematically biased label yields the best learned controller in the study.
Policy: $96.7\%$ of the surviving gap to the best available controller is control
design rather than approximation. Alongside these, supervision cuts held-out
second-derivative relative RMSE from $0.410$ to $0.045$ where three controls
carrying no curvature information fail, and holds reference-level decision cost
across a hundredfold range of a training weight that moves the unsupervised baseline
by $43\%$ while forming the largest single term in its own objective.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A neural surrogate for a parametric differential equation is trained to match a
value, but automatic differentiation returns its derivatives in the same forward
pass, and those derivatives are increasingly the product rather than a diagnostic.
Sensitivities drive design optimisation, control, and risk decisions, and they are
consumed at parameters and states chosen by the downstream procedure rather than by
whoever trained the surrogate. Standard training nevertheless controls only the
value in $L^{2}$, which permits a network to fit values closely while the slope and
curvature of the fitted field remain biased. Supervising the derivatives directly is
established practice, as Sobolev training \citep{czarnecki2017sobolev} and, in the
financial instance, as differential machine learning
\citep{huge2020differential}. What is not established is whether the resulting
derivative fields survive off-distribution, and whether the accuracy that papers
report predicts the quality of the decision the derivatives are used for.

Both ends of the relevant literature name the gap without closing it.
\citet{noguer2026derivative} proves that the error of a discretely rebalanced
decision driven by a surrogate is governed at leading order by the surrogate's
second derivative and shows that supervising the first does not reliably control the
second, but reports no decision experiment and uses a fixed-feature ridge surrogate
for its stochastic-volatility case. From the residual end,
\citet{chakhoyan2025deepsvm} report that a physics-informed operator network attains
low residual loss on this same parametric problem while producing oscillatory first
derivatives and locally negative second derivatives, finds a soft positivity penalty
insufficient, and names explicit derivative regularisation as the necessary
direction. The nearest trained configuration \citep{sakuma2026a} supervises the
first and second derivatives alongside a residual, but evaluates its decision under
the same dynamics used for training and with no costs charged, obtaining nearly
indistinguishable outcomes across arms. Whether derivative supervision changes the
decision under misspecified dynamics, and through which mechanism, has not been
tested.

This paper reports that test, and its pre-registered confirmatory contrast fails;
the failure is stated first and unhedged in Section~\ref{sec:reg}. What the design
does establish is separable from it, and is stated as arm levels rather than as
differences. Supervising the derivatives makes the held-out fields far more
accurate, and the gain is attributable to the information in the labels: three
controls that resemble derivative supervision while carrying no correct second-order
information fail to reproduce it, and a curvature penalty with no labels is worse
than adding nothing. On the decision, the supervised surrogate attains the reference controller's own
cost and holds it across a hundredfold range of the weight on the residual term, a
hyperparameter with no principled setting, while that range moves the unsupervised
baseline by $43\%$. The obvious objection, that a network trained on reference
derivatives should be expected to match reference decisions, is answered by where
the evaluation happens: the evaluated regimes are excised from training \emph{and}
validation, one lies outside the sampled range entirely, and the decision trajectory
leaves the labelled region as it runs (Figure~\ref{fig:coverage}a). The arms without
labels fail at exactly those points, so ``it was trained on the answer'' does not
survive contact with the controls.

Two findings about measurement sit alongside, and they are why the registered
scorecard reads as it does. First, the standard way of reporting derivative accuracy
scores it on a parameter grid, while the decision consumes derivatives on a
trajectory occupying a small and different part of the domain; restricted to that
region the identical metric reverses the ranking of two arms, in every seed and at
both held-out regimes. Second, the reference the decision is scored against is not
the best available controller: a variance-minimising combination of the surrogate's
own two first derivatives beats it, and the supervised arms reconstruct that
combination to within the pre-registered economic floor while a value-only network
does not. Neither finding depends on anything specific to option pricing, and we conjecture
that both recur wherever a surrogate's derivatives are scored on a grid and spent on
a trajectory; we have tested that in one setting, so this is an instrumented
existence proof with a mechanism and a protocol rather than a claim about surrogates
in general.

\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:method}

The object of study is a neural surrogate for the solution of a parametric partial
differential equation, and the quantities of interest are its derivatives. We use
option pricing under the Heston model \citep{heston1993closed} as the instance,
because it supplies what such a study requires: a semi-analytic reference for the
solution and for every derivative of it, a parameter space over which held-out
generalisation is well defined, and a downstream decision with a scalar cost.
Table~\ref{tab:notation} in Appendix~\ref{app:config} translates the notation
once; the reader who prefers the differential-equation reading can substitute
throughout.

The solution $C(S, v, \tau)$ solves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hestonpde}
\partial_\tau C=\tfrac12 vS^{2}\partial_{SS}C+\rho\xi vS\,\partial_{Sv}C
+\tfrac12\xi^{2}v\,\partial_{vv}C+(r-q)S\,\partial_{S}C
+\kappa(\theta-v)\,\partial_{v}C-rC,
\end{equation}
with the non-smooth initial condition $C(S,v,0)=(S-K)^{+}$. A single surrogate
$C_\phi(S,K,\tau;\vartheta)$ takes all eight inputs, so the coefficients
$\vartheta=(\kappa,\theta,\xi,\rho,v_0)$ are inputs rather than fixed and held-out
generalisation is a statement about unseen coefficients. Of these, $v_0$ is not a
coefficient but the \emph{current} variance, i.e.\ the state $v$ that the
$\partial_v$ derivatives of \eqref{eq:hestonpde} act on; during hedging that slot is
fed the pathwise $v_t$. Automatic differentiation
gives $\Delta=\partial_S C_\phi$, the first derivative in the state the decision
acts on, $\Gamma=\partial_{SS}C_\phi$ its curvature, and $\nu=\partial_{v_0}C_\phi$
the derivative in the second state, while the squared residual of
\eqref{eq:hestonpde} at collocation points gives the physics-informed loss
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{pde}}$ \citep{raissi2019physics}. Every arm is a flag setting
of one objective,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:loss}
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{price}}
+\lambda_{\mathrm{pde}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{pde}}
+\lambda_{D}\mathcal{L}_{D}+\lambda_{G}\mathcal{L}_{G}
+\lambda_{\nu}\mathcal{L}_{\nu},
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{L}_{D},\mathcal{L}_{G},\mathcal{L}_{\nu}$ are scale-normalised
mean-squared errors against oracle labels, the second-derivative scale frozen once so
that every arm shares it. The arms form a supervision ladder under one architecture, sampler, compute budget
and terminal treatment: value only, value plus residual (the unsupervised baseline),
then $+\Delta$, $+\Delta{+}\Gamma$, $+\Delta{+}\Gamma{+}\nu$, a residual-free
supervised arm, and the controls of Section~\ref{sec:delivers}. Five seeds per arm,
ten at the confirmatory pair and on every rung of the residual-weight sweep;
Table~\ref{tab:arms} gives the grid. The unsupervised baseline follows the physics-informed formulation of
\citet{hainaut2024option} and is called the residual-only arm below, since the
residual is its only source of derivative information. Its degradation in
Section~\ref{sec:readout} is a property of our normalisation of that residual and of
our weight-selection grid, not of the formulation as published, so we label the arm
by its configuration rather than by its source throughout. Terminal treatment is held identical because hard-constrained constructions
\citep{lagaris1998artificial} embed the non-smooth initial condition into every
derivative field, and any asymmetry there would confound the comparison.



Reference values for the solution and for each of its derivatives come from a
numerical oracle: four independently implemented solvers cross-validated to
$10^{-3}$ relative agreement, with points where they disagree above tolerance masked
under a rule declared before training and checked for neutrality across arms. Two of
the four share machinery and are not independent witnesses, which bounds what the
cross-validation certifies; the construction, the leg-by-leg diagnosis of a bias
found in one solver, and the mask panels are in Appendix~\ref{app:oracle}, and
Appendix~\ref{app:config} carries the frozen configuration in full. Every accuracy
statement below is relative to that reference.

Two scope conditions bound every claim. All five named evaluation regimes are
excised from training \emph{and} validation by relative-radius balls ($10\%$ of the unit-cube diagonal), so the
well-specified cell tests generalisation to unseen coefficients rather than the
easier task of coping with correct dynamics, and the second held-out regime lies
beyond the sampled range entirely. And the training labels are $512$ coefficient
draws crossed with $64$ shared $(S,K,\tau)$ triples, none inside the region the
decision occupies: the trajectory starts $0.011$ from the nearest labelled point in
range-normalised coordinates and ends $0.169$ away
(Figure~\ref{fig:coverage}a), so the decision is evaluated under extrapolation in
the contract coordinates as well as the coefficients. Residual collocation is
redrawn over the full domain each epoch and is exempt from the second condition.

Two kinds of held-out evaluation appear below. In the well-specified cells the
generating coefficients are those the surrogate is conditioned on and the test is
generalisation to coefficients excised from training; in the misspecified cells the
trajectories come from perturbed or jump-augmented dynamics while the surrogate
stays conditioned on the training coefficients, which is misspecification proper. At
decision time every arm receives the state and the training coefficients, never the
true ones, and the reference controller is exact for the training coefficients only,
so in the misspecified cells all of them are wrong about the world and differ only
in how well they represent the assumed model.

The downstream decision is a discrete sequential control problem, held fixed across
arms so that derivative quality is the only varying factor. A controller holding
$\hat{\Delta}$ units of the state rebalances at each of $43$ steps to a horizon
$T' = 0.17$ and settles against the true dynamics; the loss is the negative of the
resulting profit and the reported statistic is $\mathrm{CVaR}_{95}$, the mean of its
worst $5\%$. Every arm faces $10{,}000$ shared trajectories per cell and pays proportional costs
at tiers $\{0, 1\%, 2\%\}$; the confirmatory cell is the combined coefficient
perturbation at $1\%$ over 10 seeds, and the accounting conventions are in
Appendix~\ref{app:config}. Three reference controllers run beside the arms: the
exact reference derivative, the variance-minimising combination
$\Delta_{\mathrm{MV}} = \partial_S C + (\rho\xi/S)\,\partial_v C$ of the two first
derivatives,%
% [CITATION NEEDED] local-risk-minimisation / minimum-variance hedge ratio:
% Foellmer-Schweizer and an empirical minimum-variance-delta study. Neither source
% is in the verified project record, so nothing is cited here rather than citing
% unread work. Obtain, read, then add both and reference them at this point.
 and a cost-aware policy suppressing small rebalances
\citep{whalley1997asymptotic}. The mechanism falsifier is excess turnover over the
exact-derivative reference on identical trajectories,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tex}
T_{\mathrm{ex}}=\sum_{i=0}^{43}\bigl|\Delta\hat{\Delta}_i\bigr|
-\sum_{i=0}^{43}\bigl|\Delta\Delta^{\circ}_i\bigr|,
\qquad
\hat{\Delta}_{-1}=\hat{\Delta}_{43}=\Delta^{\circ}_{-1}=\Delta^{\circ}_{43}=0,
\end{equation}
so the sum charges the initial acquisition and the final liquidation as well as
the $42$ interior rebalances, and $T_{\mathrm{ex}} < 0$ denotes an arm trading less
than the oracle. A gap present
at zero cost indicates the robustness channel, a gap near zero at zero cost that
widens with cost while $T_{\mathrm{ex}}$ falls indicates the cost channel, an
unmoved $T_{\mathrm{ex}}$ rejects the cost channel whatever the profit and loss
shows, and neither anywhere is the pre-registered null.

The acceptance block, frozen before training and reproduced in
Appendix~\ref{app:thresholds}, requires a $\geq 10\%$ relative improvement with an
interval excluding zero at the confirmatory cell, rung 2 to beat rung 1 there,
demonstrated dose-response monotonicity, held-out second-derivative and second-state
RMSE each to fall by $\geq 15\%$ at value parity, and a $2\%$ negligibility floor on
every contrast. Verdicts separate a pass or a fail from a \emph{null}, where the artifact was
legitimately absent, and an \emph{error}, where evaluation was attempted and failed,
since merging the two would let a broken run read as an absence of effect. Every
contrast reports the registered pooled bootstrap over shared trajectories together
with a per-seed companion asking whether the treatment replicates across training
runs, which is what any generalisation language rests on. A pre-registered diagnostic established before training that the design could
resolve a second-derivative error of the baseline's size and not one of the
supervised arm's (Appendix~\ref{app:oracle}). That diagnostic certifies detectability
under a noise-corruption error model; the baseline's realised error turned out to be
a structured slope deficit rather than noise, so it bounds what the design can see
for the model it assumed, not for the error that occurred. A twelve-component audit of
the settlement, sign, CRN, premium, terminal-mark, residual, comparator-sourcing,
label-construction, and shared-Greek-code paths found no defect that inverts a
reported contrast. The residual operator itself is verified by manufactured
solutions rather than by shape and finiteness assertions: it vanishes on the
forward, the discount bond, and their combination, each an exact solution of the
operator, and reproduces the analytic value on a quadratic whose four independent
coefficients probe the $\tau$, $S$, $v$, and cross terms separately. Flipping the
sign of the correlation term or deleting the variance-diffusion term fails that
check, while a finiteness assertion passes under both.

The shared residual weight $\lambda_{\mathrm{pde}}$ was selected on validation only,
as the value minimising mean normalised validation RMSE over the value and its three
derivatives for the unsupervised arm over the grid $\{0, 10^{-2}, 10^{-1}, 1\}$,
returning $10^{-2}$. Two properties of that procedure matter for
Section~\ref{sec:readout}. The winner is the smallest non-zero candidate and beats
$\lambda_{\mathrm{pde}} = 0$ by only $3.4\%$, so the grid selected at its own
boundary and $(0, 10^{-2})$ was never explored. And the residual is normalised by $\mathrm{mean}[(rC^{\star})^{2}]$, the
squared discount term evaluated on the labels: model-free, but our design choice
rather than a canonical one, and degenerate as $r \to 0$. Its root mean square is
$0.527$ while the residual's own dominant term is $4.674$, so a nominal weight
carries roughly $79$ times the weight a scale-matched normalisation would give it,
and at the selected value the residual is $49\%$ of the unsupervised arm's total
loss. The contract further pre-registered a robustness row re-running the
confirmatory contrast with the weight sourced from the treated arm instead, so that
the choice ``can be shown to be immaterial to the headline result (or shown not to
be)''. That value is $0$, and the row is reported rather than deferred.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

The registered outcomes come first. The rest of the section is organised by one
decomposition: a controller's regret against the best available controller splits
into approximation error, which derivative supervision addresses, and control-design
error, which it cannot. Read through that split the registered scorecard
mis-attributes on three axes, and each is measured rather than argued below: where
the accuracy is measured (Section~\ref{sec:region}), which direction the residual
error points (Section~\ref{sec:mvbuild}), and which policy the cost tier rewards
(Section~\ref{sec:readout}).

\subsection{Pre-registered outcomes}
\label{sec:reg}

Table~\ref{tab:verdicts} reports every registered endpoint against its frozen
threshold. The confirmatory test fails: at the registered cell the relative
$\mathrm{CVaR}_{95}$ improvement of rung 3 over the residual-only baseline is $+0.02\%$
against a $10\%$ bar, with pooled and per-seed intervals both covering zero, so the
contrast is neither pooled-significant nor seed-robust. The order-attribution
endpoint, which carries the registered causal claim, also fails, and its failure is
not a null: the add-gamma rung is a reversal of $+0.0574$ in loss units, pooled
interval $[+0.0527, +0.0621]$ and per-seed interval $[+0.0311, +0.0856]$, both
excluding zero on the harmful side. Its relative size is $1.2\%$, below the
registered $2\%$ negligibility floor, so the reading is that gamma supervision is
statistically real, economically negligible, and harmful in sign at that cell,
rather than inert. The registered vocabulary, admitting only a pass and a
``failure equals honest null'', cannot express that outcome, which is recorded as a
defect of the vocabulary.

\subsection{What derivative supervision delivers}
\label{sec:delivers}

Held-out derivative accuracy is the clearest effect in the study and does not depend
on any choice examined later. Table~\ref{tab:greeks} reports held-out RMSE for all
three derivatives at both evaluation regimes, against two references: the
residual-only baseline the contract registers, and a value-only network carrying
neither residual nor derivative labels. Supervision cuts second-derivative and
second-state RMSE by $78$ to $96\%$ against either reference, attributable to the
second-derivative labels specifically, since that rung alone cuts held-out
second-derivative RMSE by $60\%$ and $59\%$, with value accuracy improving
alongside rather than being traded away.

Three controls establish that the gain is the curvature \emph{information} in the
labels rather than the smoothing effect of a curvature-shaped penalty
(Figure~\ref{fig:controls}). A label-free curvature $L^{2}$ penalty reaches held-out
second-derivative relative RMSE of $0.982$ against the unsupervised baseline's
$0.410$, worse than adding nothing; shuffled labels, preserving the label
distribution while destroying its correspondence to the state, reach $0.408$,
indistinguishable from adding nothing; and buying more value data instead of labels
saturates at $0.699$ held out, flat under a doubled-width retrain. Supervision
reaches $0.045$. The first two also degrade the first-derivative fit, so their
failure mode is training conflict rather than absence of signal, which is why they
evidence that correct curvature information is required and not a claim about
regularisers in general.

On the decision the result is best stated as a level rather than as a contrast.
Figure~\ref{fig:lambda} and Table~\ref{tab:lambda} report the two confirmatory arms
separately over a hundredfold range of the shared residual weight. The supervised
arm attains $\mathrm{CVaR}_{95}$ between $2.0758$ and $2.0905$ at every weight
including zero, against the exact-derivative reference's $2.0794$ on the same
trajectories, every rung seed-robust, while the unsupervised baseline moves from
$2.1370$ to $3.0518$ over the same range. That flatness is not an artefact of the
residual being negligible in the supervised objective. Decomposing the final
training loss, the $\lambda$-weighted residual is $53.3\%$ of rung 3's objective at
the registered weight, larger than its three derivative terms combined ($46.2\%$)
and larger than the same term's share of the unsupervised arm's objective
($42.8\%$): one term dominates both objectives and moves only one outcome. Supervision therefore reaches
reference-level performance at held-out points and holds it against a hyperparameter
with no principled setting, one that moves the unsupervised arm by more than the
treatment is worth. Section~\ref{sec:method} gave the reasons this is
generalisation rather than recall, and the controls above make the point
empirically: the arms without labels fail at these same points. At positive cost
tiers the comparison becomes cost-dominated rather than accuracy-dominated, and the
best arm is then a cost-aware policy applied to the worst derivative field in the
study (Appendix~\ref{app:exhibits}).

\subsection{Why the contrast is not a property of the treatment}
\label{sec:readout}

Read as a difference instead of a level, the same sweep behaves quite differently,
and the difference is what the contract registers. The contrast is monotone in
$\lambda_{\mathrm{pde}}$ and convex in its logarithm, spanning an order of magnitude
with no interior optimum and no plateau, and the registered weight sits where the
curve is steepest. Since the supervised arm does not move, the contrast measures the
baseline's handicap. At $\lambda_{\mathrm{pde}} = 0$ the two arms are bitwise
identical to the registered value-only and residual-free arms (maximum
$|\Delta\mathrm{CVaR}| = 0.0$ over five common seeds), so the sweep also completes
the residual-by-supervision factorial at ten seeds: the supervision effect is
$0.9614$ in loss units with the residual and $0.0611$ without it, an interaction of
$15.7$ times, and $93.6\%$ of the registered contrast is repair of damage the
residual does to the baseline. Against the undamaged value-only control, the only
reference the residual has not degraded, supervision is worth $0.0611$ in loss units at zero
cost, $2.9\%$ relative, pooled interval $[-0.0672, -0.0588]$ and seed-robust: above
the pre-registered $2\%$ floor, and an order of magnitude below the contrast at the
registered weight. Two independently written
drivers produced the curve and agree to $0.00\mathrm{e}{+}00$ at all three
overlapping rungs on bit-identical checkpoints.

The registered weight is also the worst of the six points under the contract's own
selection criterion, more than twice the score at the optimum near $10^{-4}$
(Table~\ref{tab:lambda}). That is a grid-resolution failure rather than a rule failure: the rule was evaluated
at four points that miss its own minimum by two orders of magnitude. A second
failure survives any refinement. On the selection region the residual helps the
baseline (validation first-derivative RMSE $0.0418$ at $10^{-2}$ against $0.0607$ at
$0$) while at the states the decision visits it hurts ($0.0989$ against $0.0484$),
so the criterion improves in the direction that degrades the decision, and since the
evaluation regimes are excised from validation, no validation point can report that.
At the registered cost tier the contrast is negative at every weight except the
registered one, where it is $+0.01\%$: the single most favourable cell in the
explored grid, and an uninformative coincidence, since the weight was fixed by a
validation-blind rule and the tiers registered before it. Its mechanism is in the
same table, the damage inflating the zero-cost gap while the damaged arm's
under-trading turns the cost tier into an offsetting subsidy. That tier is also roughly $200$ times the rate
\citet{armstrong2024deep} calibrate for the traded instrument, a design error
discovered after freezing, and it licenses no rescue: at the criterion's own optimum
the contrast at their stress tier is $-0.30\%$, so any cost-tier reframing is
withdrawn rather than relabelled.

What the residual does is measurable on the derivative field itself, and it is the
mechanism of the damage rather than a benefit of supervision. On the decision
trajectories the baseline's first derivative is a flattened sigmoid, regression slope
$0.637$ against the reference and a state-dependent bias of $+0.101$, $-0.029$, and
$-0.107$ across the three moneyness regions: a slope deficit, not a level bias. It
therefore under-trades the reference by $38\%$ ($T_{\mathrm{ex}} = -0.695$) while
supervision restores reference-level turnover ($-0.007$), so the registered cost
channel runs against the treatment, its falsifier having presumed an over-trading
baseline. The registered robustness channel fares no better: $86\%$ of the zero-cost contrast
is already present under correct dynamics and the effect decays with misspecification
severity, from $+27.7\%$ to $-4.2\%$ across the jump sweep. The account consistent
with every cell is approximation quality of the first derivative feeding
discrete-time tracking error, which \citet{noguer2026derivative} bounds in theory
and for which the registered two-by-two has no bin; the pre-registered
well-specified cell detected the omission (Appendix~\ref{app:exhibits}).

\subsection{Where accuracy is measured, and against which hedge}
\label{sec:region}

The registered accuracy metric is scored on the full grid ($S \in [50,150]$,
$K \in [60,140]$, $\tau \in [0.04,1]$) while the decision runs at one contract
coordinate over $\tau \in [0.08, 0.25]$. Restricting the identical metric to the
region the decision occupies (Table~\ref{tab:greeks},
Figure~\ref{fig:coverage}b) reverses the residual-only baseline against the
value-only network at both held-out regimes, on both derivatives, in all five seeds;
the baseline is the only arm that degrades on entering it, first-derivative RMSE
rising from $0.0537$ to $0.0937$ while every other arm improves. The residual's
dominant term $\tfrac12 vS^{2}\partial_{SS}C$ is stiffest near the non-smooth
initial condition and its root mean square is $1.45$ times larger inside the region,
consistent with the reversal without establishing it. The consequence is
method-level: two models can rank one way under the protocol the literature reports
and the opposite way under the accuracy the decision consumes. The axes do not
conflict when measured in one region, where first-derivative RMSE orders the arms
($0.0038 < 0.0057 < 0.0202 < 0.0484 < 0.0989 < 0.1458$) exactly as zero-cost
$\mathrm{CVaR}_{95}$ does, with two departures Section~\ref{sec:mvbuild} explains.

A ranking reversal is an existence result, and on its own it does not license the
obvious recommendation. Tested directly, restricting the metric to the decision's
region is barely better than the grid at predicting realised cost: pooled pairwise
concordance over seven arms, five seeds and two held-out regimes is $0.733$
restricted against $0.710$ on the grid, $0.860$ against $0.835$ once the
biased-label arm is set aside, and for the first derivative at the extrapolated
regime the restricted metric is worse ($\rho$ $0.271$ against $0.350$). The region
is the wrong object. What it crudely approximates is the error weighted by the
occupancy measure the controller induces,
$\mathbb{E}_{\mu^{\pi}}[(\hat{D}C - DC)^{2}]$, evaluated at the states at which the
controller actually forms positions; recomputed on the frozen trajectories, that
quantity predicts realised cost better than either alternative on every derivative
and on both statistics (Table~\ref{tab:metric}). The recommendation is therefore the
estimand, with the region-restricted metric named as its crude proxy rather than as
the fix. Two caveats travel with it. The occupancy metric differs from the other two
in weighting \emph{and} in parameter cell, so only the grid-versus-region comparison
is clean; and it is one cell over five seeds, which makes it suggestive rather than
settled. A third bounds the in-region accuracy claim itself: label support inside
that region is thin, masked at $1.30$ times the outside rate, with two maturity
nodes the decision passes through masked at $98.8\%$ and $84.8\%$
(Appendix~\ref{app:exhibits}).

\subsection{Reconstructing the better controller from the supervised fields}
\label{sec:mvbuild}

The reference the decision is scored against is not the best available controller.
Driving the same trajectories with $\Delta_{\mathrm{MV}}$ gives misspecified
zero-cost $\mathrm{CVaR}_{95}$ of $1.670$ against the exact-derivative reference's
$2.082$, $19.8\%$ better, and $10.6\%$ better under correct dynamics. The registered
headline statistic, the fraction of the baseline-to-reference gap closed, therefore
presupposes a ceiling that is not one: the supervised arm closes $98.6\%$ of the
distance to the exact derivative but $69.8\%$ of the distance to the controller that
wins. As a decomposition, rung 3's regret against the best
available controller is $0.4261$ in loss units, of which $0.0141$ is approximation
error against the exact derivative and $0.4120$ is the design of the
exact-derivative policy itself: $96.7\%$ of what is left to win is control design, a
ratio of $29.2$ to one. For the unsupervised arm the split inverts to $70.8\%$
approximation, which is what being the damaged arm looks like. This is the sense in
which the registered decision metric could not have rewarded accuracy at the
accuracy levels supervision reaches. This also explains the two departures noted above and
the registered label-noise outcome: a systematically biased second-derivative label
produces the best learned controller in the study at zero cost, because its bias
lies in the variance-minimising direction and recovers $87\%$ of that gap
(Appendix~\ref{app:exhibits}).

Because $\Delta_{\mathrm{MV}}$ is assembled from two first derivatives,
$\partial_S C$ and $\partial_v C$, any arm carrying both can build it from its own
outputs with no further reference. This is where the accuracy result and the
decision result meet. It was run after the registered analysis was complete and is exploratory: it moves
no registered verdict and is labelled as such wherever it appears. It is not,
however, an anecdote. It reuses the frozen checkpoints, the same
common-random-number trajectories every other arm hedges, and the same paired
bootstrap the contract registers, at five seeds.

Every arm that supervises the first two derivatives reconstructs the
variance-minimising controller to within the pre-registered $2\%$ floor
(Table~\ref{tab:mv}, Appendix~\ref{app:exhibits}): rung 3 lands at $1.6701$ against
the analytic $1.6696$, a pooled difference of $+0.0004$ with interval
$[-0.0013, +0.0021]$ covering zero, and rung 2 at $1.6563$, marginally better than
the analytic construction and seed-robust but at $0.80\%$ relative, inside the
floor. The arms without second-derivative labels do not: supervising only the first
derivative leaves $3.27\%$ and the value-only network $3.40\%$, both seed-robust and
both outside the floor, while the residual-damaged baseline leaves $42.9\%$. Two readings follow. Derivative accuracy transfers to a better decision once the
decision consumes the fields supervision improves, which the registered
first-derivative-only decision never did: the reconstructed controller beats the
exact-derivative reference by $19.8\%$ misspecified and $10.6\%$ under correct
dynamics, both seed-robust. And second-derivative supervision is sufficient for the reconstruction without
second-state supervision: the step from first-derivative-only to first-and-second
closes the gap ($-3.27\%$ to $+0.80\%$, both seed-robust) while adding the
second-state label on top changes nothing outside the floor. Necessity is not
established, because the ladder never trained a first-plus-second-state arm, which
is the obvious control and the first experiment we would run next. The mechanism is in Table~\ref{tab:greeks}: supervising the second
derivative also improves the \emph{unsupervised} second-state derivative that
$\Delta_{\mathrm{MV}}$ consumes, from $2.66$ to $1.59$. This is the one result that
gives the registered causal rung a role, and it is a role on a different decision
from the registered one.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Every registered efficacy endpoint of this study failed, and the apparent effect
size on the registered decision is not a property of the treatment at all: it is a
monotone readout of the residual weight, $93.6\%$ of it repair of damage that
residual does to the baseline in exactly the region the decision occupies. What the design establishes instead is where that scorecard sends the credit wrong.
Accuracy is scored on a grid while the decision consumes it on a trajectory, and the
error weighted by the controller's own occupancy measure predicts the decision
better than the grid or a hand-drawn region. Direction matters as much as size,
which is why a biased label produces the best learned controller here. And $96.7\%$
of the gap still separating the supervised arm from the best available controller is
control design, so at the accuracy supervision reaches this decision could not have
rewarded accuracy.
Supervision itself does deliver: held-out derivative accuracy that three
information-free controls cannot reproduce, reference-level decision cost held
across a hundredfold range of a training weight that is simultaneously the largest
term in its own objective, and enough fidelity in two first derivatives to
reconstruct a variance-minimising controller that beats the exact-derivative
reference by $19.8\%$.

What we would ask of any study reporting derivative accuracy for a surrogate whose
derivatives are spent downstream. Score the derivative error against the occupancy measure the decision induces rather
than on the parameter grid alone, and do not settle for restricting the grid to a
hand-drawn region: in our data the two regions ranked two arms oppositely in every
seed, yet the restriction bought almost nothing in predictive power while the
occupancy estimand bought a great deal. Report treated and control arms as levels, not only
as contrasts, because a contrast moves when either arm moves and ours moved
elevenfold while the treated arm sat still. Resolve hyperparameter grids finely
enough to locate their own optimum; ours selected at its boundary and the registered
value was the worst of six points under the criterion that chose it. Check that the
reference the decision is scored against is the best one available, since ours was
not. And fix a negligibility floor in advance, because pooled resolution over
$10^{4}$ trajectories finds statistically real effects that are worth nothing.

Five limitations bound these claims, the first sharpest. The magnitude of the
residual damage depends on our own normalisation of that term, which inflates its
effective weight by roughly $79$ times, so the size of the pathology should not be
read as generic; what generalises in form is the pair of selection failures, of
which only the first is repaired by a finer grid. The second-derivative rung was not re-measured at a weight the criterion endorses.
The controls, the reconstruction result, and the residual-free arm carry five seeds
rather than ten, and the occupancy comparison is one parameter cell. The decision
terminates at $\tau = 0.08$, so the null is scoped to that horizon rather than
showing the design blind to curvature effects, which also act earlier. Label support
inside the decision's region is thin, and the accuracy claim there is scored on a
mask that removes $1.30$ times as many points as outside it. Labels and reference
come from the same oracle family, so this is generalisation of a solution field
rather than discovery of an unknown one; the surrogate is a small smooth tanh
network on a two-state parabolic problem, not a claim about surrogates in general;
and the misspecification is synthetic, with the second held-out regime lying
$0.101$ in $\rho$ beyond the sampled edge, about a sixth of that coordinate's range,
a real but modest extrapolation. The natural next experiments are the missing
first-plus-second-state arm, supervising toward $\Delta_{\mathrm{MV}}$ directly,
rebalancing finely enough that the discretisation term the theory identifies binds,
and dosing label bias rather than label variance.

\section{Cost profile, dose-response, and supporting exhibits}
\label{app:exhibits}

\paragraph{What the mask removes, and where.} The oracle mask drops $9.79\%$ of
label cells. It does not select the hard points: masked cells have lower mean
curvature than retained ones ($0.0042$ against $0.0091$) and sit at short maturity
and deep out-of-the-money, where a \emph{relative} agreement tolerance is hardest to
satisfy, so the mask is a tolerance artefact rather than difficulty selection.
It is not neutral in location, however. Inside the region the decision occupies the
mask rate is $0.1233$ against $0.0948$ outside, a ratio of $1.30$, and two maturity
nodes the trajectory passes through are almost entirely removed, $98.8\%$ at
$\tau = 0.093$ and $84.8\%$ at $\tau = 0.115$. The in-region accuracy claim of
Section~\ref{sec:region} is therefore scored where label support is thinnest, which
is stated with the claim rather than in a footnote.

\paragraph{What derivative labels cost.} Derivative labels are near-free from the
characteristic-function leg and ruinous from the grid solver, which matters for
anyone deciding whether to buy them. Measured on one machine over $64$ contract
points, single process, so ratios transfer and seconds do not: analytic
differentiation through the characteristic-function integrand costs $1.0$ times a
price call, since one quadrature serves the value and every derivative;
finite-difference stencils on the transform prices cost $8.4$ times; the
Monte-Carlo leg $1.9$ times at a tenth of production paths, so roughly $19$ times at
production; and the Craig--Sneyd solve $688$ times. Read against the
information-matching plateau, which puts derivative labels at roughly three times
their value-point budget in finite-difference-equivalent units, labels pay for
themselves from the cheap leg by a wide margin, and the expensive leg is required
only for the Feller-stressed band.

the turnover diagnostics, and Table~\ref{tab:dose} the gamma-label dose-response on
both of its axes. Two features of Table~\ref{tab:tiers} are load-bearing for
Section~\ref{sec:readout}. At and above the registered tier the best arm is a
no-trade band applied to the worst delta in the study, and the price-only network
beats both the supervised arms and the oracle delta, so the cost regime rewards
under-trading rather than accuracy. No cost-aware supervised arm was run, and the
paper is therefore silent on what to deploy there. The band width was itself
selected at the boundary of its registered grid, which is the same
selection-resolution pattern that Section~\ref{sec:readout} documents for the

\section{Oracle validation, mask-neutrality panels, and the headroom ladder}
\label{app:oracle}

\paragraph{Construction.} The primary leg differentiates the trap-free
characteristic-function branch of \citet{albrecher2007little}, with Fourier pricing
per \citet{carrmadan1999option}, before discretisation rather than after; the second
takes high-order finite differences on the same price grid, the third is Monte-Carlo
pathwise, and the fourth a Craig--Sneyd ADI solver routed to points where the
variance process is stressed. The first two share the characteristic-function
machinery, so the four legs are three independent witnesses at most.

\paragraph{Fourth-leg diagnosis.} On \texttt{feller\_violating\_volvol}, the ADI
leg's vega disagreed with the characteristic-function leg systematically, exceeding
it on $87.9\%$ of grid points, growing monotonically in maturity from $0.0$ at
$\tau = 0.04$ to $0.0087$ at $\tau = 1.0$, and masking $59.4\%$ of that regime's
vega grid. Four candidate causes were eliminated in order
(Table~\ref{tab:adi}). The survivor was variance-domain truncation: at the previous
default the variance domain is cut inside the mass of the $\xi = 0.60$ regime and
the artificial boundary contaminates $v$-direction derivatives. Raising $v_{\max}$
from $1.6$ to $3.2$ changes the median disagreement from $4.0\mathrm{e}{-}05$ to
$2.8\mathrm{e}{-}05$, so $1.6$ is converged, and \texttt{near\_feller} is flat under
the same sweep, which localises the effect to broad-$\xi$ regimes. The worst
relative error on the affected regime improved from $3.56\mathrm{e}{-}03$ to
$1.57\mathrm{e}{-}03$ against a $5\mathrm{e}{-}03$ bar. What adjudicated the
disagreement was narrower than four legs suggests: the Monte-Carlo band exceeded the
disagreement on $85\%$ of points and could not break the tie. On that regime the
vega uncertainty is $1.33$, dominated by Monte-Carlo noise at $\xi = 0.60$ and
binding on $70\%$ of points, which no solver change lowers.

\paragraph{Mask-neutrality panels.} The Feller-band panel shows the affected band
halving from $0.5192$ to $0.2434$ while every other band is bit-identical, the
control confirming that the change touched only the fourth leg. That band remains
elevated against roughly $0.09$ elsewhere, its excess falling from $5.8\times$ to
$2.7\times$. The marginal-distribution panel moved in both directions: $\kappa$,
$\theta$, and $\xi$ improved ($0.0645 \to 0.0287$, $0.1787 \to 0.1201$,
$0.1284 \to 0.0383$), while $\rho$ and $v_0$ worsened ($0.1242 \to 0.1492$,
$0.0832 \to 0.1266$). The improvement in $\theta$ and $\xi$ is mechanical, since the
Feller ratio is $2\kappa\theta/\xi^{2}$ and band-concentrated masking is itself a
$\theta$ and $\xi$ shift; the deterioration in $\rho$ and $v_0$ follows from the
masked set shrinking by roughly $12\%$ and changing composition. The fix is
therefore not a uniform improvement and is not presented as one. The
curvature-decile panel improved at every decile including the highest
($0.1221 \to 0.1010$), which is the one that matters for gamma supervision, and the
contract's stated failure mode for that panel, the mask rate rising with curvature,
does not occur.

\paragraph{Headroom-gate ladder.} Rungs at
$\sigma_{\mathrm{rel}} \in \{0.05, 0.10, 0.15\}$ are decision rungs;
$\{0.20, 0.40\}$ are diagnostic, swept and plotted identically but excluded from the
decision scan (Table~\ref{tab:ladder}). The bound
$\texttt{clipped\_frac\_max} = 0.25$ was not raised to re-admit $0.20$, whose
per-seed maximum straddles it, since a rung whose eligibility depends on which seeds
were drawn is not a decision rung. The bound is structural rather than statistical:
above it the corrupted arm is a saturated hedger pinned at the delta bound over much
of the state space, whose gamma error is the oracle's own $-\Gamma$ rather than the
calibrated field, and no trained network's Greek error behaves that way. Both pilots
reported in Section~\ref{sec:method} lie inside the region of validity, at clipped
fractions of $0.063$ and $0.000$.
