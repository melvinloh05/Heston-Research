01

What changed, and what the audit was asked to find

The 18 August dossier reported that every registered endpoint had a verdict and that four of eight fail. That still stands. This revision answers a different and sharper question, put by the adviser after reading the methods draft beside the results: is there a bug in the collection code that inverted every directional prediction?

No such bug exists. Twelve components were re-derived against the frozen artifacts rather than read — settlement and self-financing recursion, CVaR sign, CRN discipline, premium and terminal-liability conventions, provider state symmetry, the PDE residual term by term, the MV comparator’s parameter sourcing, the dose labels, and the Greek code path shared by grid and hedge evaluation. All correct. The numbers are what the code was asked to compute (§13–14, and docs/CODE_AUDIT_2026-08-20.md §1).

The audit instead found two design defects, and the first of them is worse than a bug would have been, because a bug can be fixed and republished whereas this removes a number from the class of things that can be quoted at all:

  1. The zero-cost headline is a readout of λPDE, not a measurement of supervision. Swept over four new values, it runs monotonically from +2.9% to +31.5% with no interior optimum and no plateau. Decomposed, rung 3 never moves (2.076→2.090 against an oracle of 2.079); the entire curve is the unsupervised baseline degrading by 43%. And the registered λPDE = 0.01 is the worst point on a fine grid re-scored with the contract’s own selection rule, which prefers ~1e−4. (§13)
  2. The two headline metrics are measured in disjoint regions of (S, K, τ) in which the arms rank oppositely. On the full OOD grid the residual-only baseline beats a price-only net 2.5×; inside the hedging box it loses 2.6×. Seed-robust, all five seeds, all anchors. (§14)

Two claims are withdrawn outright as a result. The cost-tier reframe of §04 does not survive the corrected λPDE — its strongest number, +14.54% at Armstrong & Tatlow’s stress tier, becomes −0.30% at the criterion’s own optimum. And “the hedging and accuracy axes move independently” is an artifact of §14: measured in one region, accuracy does predict hedging. §11’s hyperparameter-sourcing finding is not withdrawn but is subsumed — it is one point on the curve in §13.

What survives is smaller, harder, and hyperparameter-independent

rung 3 attains oracle-level delta-only CVaR95 at every λPDE tested including zero (2.076–2.090 vs oracle 2.079), seed-robust. It is the one quantity in the study that does not move when the knob moves, and it should lead. Out-of-distribution Greek accuracy also survives, reported beside its region (§14). The three falsification controls and the re-tested Sakuma null are untouched by this audit.

Read order for an adjudicator: §13 and §14 first — they change what §04, §05, §07 and §11 are allowed to say. §15 carries the resulting claim-status table and the open items.

02

The pre-training power check passed — but the arm chosen decides it

What the headroom gate is for

Before any arm was trained, it hedges the confirmatory instrument twice — once with exact oracle Greeks, once with oracle Greeks corrupted by a field calibrated to a plausible network's gamma error. The spread between the two P&L distributions is a ceiling on any effect the design could detect.

Objection targeted: “a null result here just means the experiment was underpowered.” The gate answers that in advance, and it authorises the compute spend.

The mechanism is neither registered channel

The registration crosses (i) model-uncertainty robustness against (ii) transaction-cost / turnover. Applied mechanically the rule returns channel (i). The in-model cell falsifies that reading:

Cell (TC = 0)rung3 vs standard PINN
In-model — no misspecification at all+27.2%
Misspecified+31.5%
 Misspecification contributes ~4pp of a 31.5pp effect.

About 86% of the effect exists with no misspecification and no costs. The dominant channel is plain Δ-estimation quality, and the registered 2×2 has no bin for it — it presumed the baseline's Greeks would be roughly serviceable. Mean PnL is identical across arms at zero cost (+0.0773 each), so this is pure tail reduction, not return.

“In-model” is not “in-distribution”

Hedging trains at the baseline anchor, and every named anchor has a 10%-relative-radius ball excised from the training sample. Measured on the frozen artifact, the nearest training point to baseline sits at L2 distance 0.2901 in range-normalised coordinates, with zero points inside the ball. So the in-model cell has no misspecification — the true dynamics are the ones the pricer assumes — but the parameter point is held out, and the network must generalise its Greek fields there. The two conditions are distinct and should not be merged in the write-up: the effect is about generalising derivatives to unseen parameters, not about coping with wrong dynamics.

Recommended reporting: execute the registered rule verbatim, print its literal output (channel_i), then immediately show the in-model cell demonstrating the dichotomy was not exhaustive. Two registration-design lessons, both owned explicitly.

Why costs invert it

The unsupervised baseline's delta surface is too flat, so it trades 38% less than the oracle (turnover 0.0248 vs 0.0398; excess turnover −0.696 against rung3's −0.007). Under proportional costs that under-trading collects a discount nobody chose — an accidental no-trade band. At 2% cost even the exact oracle loses to it by 20%, which is Whalley–Wilmott behaving exactly as theory predicts, not a defect.

Cross-model transfer: Bates and Merton

The effect decays monotonically with jump severity and dies once the data-generating process is far enough from Heston.

What the Bates sweep is for

It hedges under a jump-diffusion process the network was never trained on, dialling jump intensity λj and jump size σj upward to move the true dynamics progressively further from the Heston model class. λj = 0 must recover the Heston result, which it does.

Objection targeted: “your misspecification is a small parameter tweak inside the same model — show what happens when the world is genuinely a different process.” It also, unexpectedly, falsifies the robustness framing: the registered hypothesis predicts the benefit should grow with misspecification, and it shrinks.

It bolts a classical Whalley–Wilmott no-trade band onto the unsupervised baseline's delta: do not rebalance unless delta has moved more than w. It is a cheap policy rule carrying no Greek information at all.

10

Loose ends

Everything registered has now been collected and analysed. Nothing is running: 85 training runs, 100 sweep cells, three confirmatory passes, band selection and re-run, Greek evaluation, the saturation sweep, the verdict tables, the ledger, and the exhibits.

Still open in the registration: whether the price-parity clause is two-sided as written or one-sided in intent; and the residual × supervision factorial, which is populated on three of four cells because no arm is price-only × PDE-ablated — feedforward ablates boundary conditions as well, so using it there would introduce a confound.

Known limitation. The information-matched baseline cannot be run at its intended budget: the saturation cap is 5N = 20,480 price points while the frozen artifact holds 23,623 rows, which the standard PINN already consumes. What the sweep does establish is that price-only Γ accuracy plateaus by m = 2 (0.0076 across a 2.5× data increase) and gets worse at 2× width — so the optimization-limited objection dies. The strongest available price-only comparator is the standard PINN itself.

One contract self-inconsistency worth knowing

headline_scale_free — “fraction of the baseline-to-oracle gap closed” — is undefined at the registered confirmatory tier, because at 1% cost the oracle is worse than the baseline and the denominator goes negative. At zero cost it is clean and monotone: rung1 90.8%, rung2 97.2%, rung3 98.9%. The contract's own headline metric cannot be computed at the contract's own confirmatory cell — the tier problem showing up in a third place.

The oracle is not the ceiling

Why this was run

A wrong-model Black–Scholes gamma label (bs_gamma) hedged better than the exact-Greek oracle, which needed either an explanation or an honest “unexplained”. Under stochastic volatility the frictionless delta is not the variance-minimising hedge ratio: spot and variance are correlated, so the minimum-variance delta adds a term, ΔMV = ∂C/∂S + (ρξ/S)·∂C/∂v.

rung 3 drifts 2.0758 → 2.0904, i.e. 0.7% across a 100× change in λPDE, and is pinned to the oracle’s 2.0794 throughout. standard_pinn degrades monotonically 2.1370 → 3.0518, +43%. The reported effect size is therefore not a measurement of what supervision achieves — that is constant, and already at the ceiling — but of how much the PDE residual damages the unsupervised baseline. There is no value of λPDE at which the effect settles, so there is no principled way to quote a single figure. “+31.5%” is not a quantity this study measured.

The registered configuration is the single most favourable point in the surface

The accuracy and hedging metrics are measured in disjoint regions where the arms rank oppositely

Why this was looked for

The results carry an apparent paradox the draft turned into a finding: the baseline is respectable on the OOD Greek table yet catastrophic at hedging. That was written up as “the two axes move independently”. Independence between two metrics computed from the same network is a strong claim, so it was checked by evaluating the same metric with the same code in the region each headline actually inhabits.

Objection targeted: “you are comparing a Greek error somewhere the hedge never goes with a P&L somewhere you never measured the Greek.”

On the full grid the residual-only baseline is 2.5× better than a price-only net. In the hedging box it is 2.6× worse. standard_pinn is the only arm that degrades moving into the slice (0.0414 → 0.0706); every other arm improves there. It is a property of the region, not of the anchor or the seed.

“The hedging and accuracy axes move independently” is withdrawn

Measured in a single region, accuracy does predict hedging. On the confirmatory paths themselves the arms order by Δ RMSE exactly as their P&L orders (rung 3 0.0038, rung 2 0.0057, sans_pde 0.0056, against oracle Δ on the same states), and the departures that remain are the hedge-ratio / minimum-variance effect of §12, which is a different and nameable thing. The independence was an artifact of the measurement design. The accuracy claim itself survives — the supervised arms win in both regions — but the interpretation built on top of it does not.

Three contributing causes, all measured

Selection is blind to the eval region

λPDE is scored on hypercube validation × full grid, where standard_pinn beats feedforward by 31% (0.0418 vs 0.0607). At hedge states the same pair reads 0.0989 vs 0.0484 — it loses by 104%. Anchors are excised from training and validation, so no selection signal ever sees the region the claim is evaluated in.

The normaliser inflates the effective λ ~79×

loss_scale_pde = mean((r·price)²) has rms 0.527, while the residual’s dominant term rms(½vS²Γ) is 4.674 — a ratio of 8.9, squared. At the registered λ the PDE term is 49% of standard_pinn’s total loss, and its in-sample price MSE is 28× worse than feedforward’s (8.64e−5 vs 3.05e−6).

Label coverage thins along the hedge’s life

64 (S, K, τ) triples are shared between the label grid and the hedge. The hedge starts on one of them (distance 0.011 at τ = 0.25) and walks away from all of them as τ falls, reaching 0.169 — with zero labels within 0.15 — by τ = 0.08.

The consequence, directly measured

standard_pinn’s Δ at hedge states is a flattened sigmoid: regression slope 0.637 on the oracle Δ, biased +0.101 OTM, −0.029 ATM, −0.107 ITM. The “38% under-trading” of §06 is this, and it is real and measured rather than inferred from turnover.

The question this revision puts to the adjudicator

The registered program returns a null, the headline effect is not identified, and the cost-tier route to a positive result is closed. What remains is a coherent and defensible pair of findings: derivative supervision buys large, seed-robust out-of-distribution Greek accuracy, and it buys hedging performance only up to the oracle — which it reaches, and which is itself ~20% short of the minimum-variance hedge (§12). Alongside them sit three methodological findings that are arguably the more transferable contribution: a validation criterion anti-correlated with the primary metric, a selection grid that terminated at its own boundary, and two headline metrics evaluated in regions that do not overlap.

The ruling needed is on scope, not on any individual number: is that a paper, or a workshop note plus a negative-results report? Both are honest. They are not the same submission.
