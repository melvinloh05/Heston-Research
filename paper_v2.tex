\begin{abstract}
Neural surrogates for parametric differential equations expose derivatives by automatic differentiation, and
downstream procedures increasingly consume those derivatives rather than only the solution. Yet evaluations rarely
test whether they remain accurate outside training or predict the decisions they drive.
Sobolev training supervises derivatives directly, while physics-informed training constrains
them indirectly through a residual; neither establishes this link off distribution. We pre-register
derivative-accuracy and sequential-decision endpoints for a parametric Heston surrogate, using a
cross-validated numerical reference and coefficient regimes excised from training and validation.
Supervision reduces held-out second-derivative relative RMSE from $0.410$ to
$0.045$, a gain not reproduced by three information-free controls. The registered downstream
endpoint nevertheless fails ($+0.02\%$ against a $10\%$ bar); against an undamaged
value-only control, the zero-cost gain is only $2.9\%$. At the supervised arm's accuracy,
$96.7\%$ of its remaining gap to the analytic minimum-variance
controller is control design rather than approximation.
An exploratory decision-cell comparison finds occupancy-weighted
derivative error ranks realised cost better than grid error, motivating evaluation
where downstream procedures consume the learned field.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A neural surrogate for a parametric differential equation is trained to match a
value, but automatic differentiation returns its derivatives in the same forward
pass, and those derivatives are increasingly the product rather than a
diagnostic. Sensitivities drive design optimisation, control, and risk
decisions, and they are consumed at parameters and states chosen by the
downstream procedure rather than by whoever trained the surrogate, whether the
surrogate is a collocation network \citep{raissi2019physics} or an operator
\citep{lu2021learning}. Standard
training nevertheless controls only the value in $L^{2}$, which permits a network
to fit values closely while the slope and curvature of the fitted field remain
biased, and adding a residual term does not remove the problem so much as move it,
since such objectives have their own documented failure modes
\citep{krishnapriyan2021characterizing}. Figure~\ref{fig:problem} shows the effect at a parameter setting held out
of training: three surrogates agree with the reference solution to within the
width of the plotted line, and their second derivatives do not agree with it at
all.

Both ends of the relevant literature name the gap without closing it. Supervising
derivatives directly is established as Sobolev training
\citep{czarnecki2017sobolev} and, in its financial instance, as differential
machine learning \citep{huge2020differential}, but the reported evaluations are
in distribution and stop at derivative accuracy.
\citet{noguer2026derivative} proves that the error of a discretely rebalanced
decision driven by a surrogate is governed at leading order by the surrogate's
second derivative, and its trained-network experiment shows that supervising the
first does not reliably control the second. It reports no downstream decision
experiment; its Heston and Bates illustrations use fixed random features with a
ridge readout. From the residual end, \citet{chakhoyan2025deepsvm} report that a
physics-informed operator network attains low residual loss on this same
parametric problem while producing oscillatory first derivatives and locally
negative second derivatives, find a soft positivity penalty insufficient, and
name explicit derivative regularisation as the necessary direction. The nearest
trained configuration \citep{sakuma2026a} supervises the first and second
derivatives alongside a residual, but evaluates its decision under the same
dynamics used for training and with no costs charged, obtaining nearly
indistinguishable outcomes across arms. Whether derivative supervision changes
the decision off-distribution, and through which mechanism, has not been tested.

We test it on a parametric surrogate for a two-state parabolic problem whose
solution and derivatives admit an independent numerical reference, so that
held-out accuracy is measurable rather than inferred, and whose standard
downstream use is a sequential control problem with a scalar cost. The design is
pre-registered: the arms, the thresholds, the evaluation regimes, and the
mechanism falsifier were frozen before training, and the evaluated regimes are
excised from training and from validation, one of them lying outside the sampled
range entirely. Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline} gives the pipeline. The registered
efficacy endpoints fail, and the same design then measures why the accuracy that
supervision delivers does not reach the decision that consumes it.

We contribute, first, a pre-registered off-distribution evaluation of derivative
supervision against a cross-validated numerical reference for the solution and each
of its derivatives, reporting arms as levels rather than only as contrasts; second,
evidence that the accuracy gain is carried by the information in the labels rather
than by the smoothing effect of a derivative-shaped penalty, established against
three controls that supply the shape without the information; third, a diagnosis of
why grid-reported derivative accuracy can mis-rank downstream performance, together
with the occupancy-weighted error that better predicts it; and fourth, an
exploratory exception to the registered task null, in which the supervised fields
reconstruct a controller outperforming the exact-derivative reference.

\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:method}

The object of study is a neural surrogate for the solution of a parametric partial
differential equation, and the quantities of interest are its derivatives. We use
option pricing under the Heston model \citep{heston1993closed} as the instance
because it supplies what such a study requires: a semi-analytic reference for the
solution and for every derivative of it, a parameter space over which held-out
generalisation is well defined, and a downstream decision with a scalar cost.
Table~\ref{tab:notation} in Appendix~\ref{app:config} translates the notation once.

The solution $u(S, v, \tau)$ solves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hestonpde}
\partial_\tau u=\tfrac12 vS^{2}\partial_{SS}u+\rho\xi vS\,\partial_{Sv}u
+\tfrac12\xi^{2}v\,\partial_{vv}u+(r-q)S\,\partial_{S}u
+\kappa(\theta-v)\,\partial_{v}u-ru,
\end{equation}
with the non-smooth initial condition $u(S,v,0)=(S-K)^{+}$. One surrogate
$u_\phi(S,K,\tau;\vartheta,v)$ takes all eight inputs, with coefficients
$\vartheta=(\kappa,\theta,\xi,\rho)$ and the current variance state $v$ supplied
rather than fixed. The implementation names the state slot $v_0$ because labels are
priced from an initial state; at decision time it is fed the realised $v_t$.
Held-out generalisation therefore concerns unseen coefficient vectors and anchor
states. Automatic differentiation returns $\partial_S u_\phi$,
the first derivative in the state the decision acts on, $\partial_{SS}u_\phi$ its
curvature, and $\partial_v u_\phi$ the derivative in the second state, while the
squared residual of \eqref{eq:hestonpde} at collocation points gives the
physics-informed loss $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{pde}}$ \citep{raissi2019physics}. Every
arm is a flag setting of one objective,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:loss}
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{value}}
+\lambda_{\mathrm{pde}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{pde}}
+\lambda_{1}\mathcal{L}_{1}+\lambda_{2}\mathcal{L}_{2}
+\lambda_{v}\mathcal{L}_{v},
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{L}_{1},\mathcal{L}_{2},\mathcal{L}_{v}$ are scale-normalised mean
squared errors against reference labels for $\partial_S u$, $\partial_{SS}u$ and
$\partial_v u$, the second-derivative scale frozen once so that every arm shares it.
The arms form a supervision ladder under one architecture, sampler, compute budget
and terminal treatment: value only, value plus residual, then $+\partial_S u$,
$+\partial_{SS}u$, $+\partial_v u$, a residual-free supervised arm, and the controls
of Section~\ref{sec:delivers}. Five seeds per arm, ten at the confirmatory pair and
on every rung of the residual-weight sweep; Table~\ref{tab:arms} gives the grid.
Terminal treatment is identical across arms because hard-constrained constructions
\citep{lagaris1998artificial} embed the non-smooth initial condition into every
derivative field, and any asymmetry there would confound the comparison. The
value-plus-residual arm follows the formulation of \citet{hainaut2024option} and is
called the residual-only arm below, since the residual is its only source of
derivative information; its degradation in Section~\ref{sec:readout} is a property
of our normalisation of that residual and of our weight-selection grid, not of the
formulation as published.

Reference values for the solution and for each of its derivatives come from four
independently implemented solvers cross-validated to $10^{-3}$ relative agreement,
with points where they disagree above tolerance masked under a rule declared before
training and checked for neutrality across arms. Two of the four share machinery and
are not independent witnesses, which bounds what the cross-validation certifies. The construction, a diagnosis of a bias found in one solver, the mask panels, a
twelve-component audit of the evaluation paths and a manufactured-solutions
verification of the residual operator are in Appendix~\ref{app:oracle}.

Two scope conditions bound every claim. All five named evaluation regimes are
excised from training \emph{and} from validation by relative-radius balls ($10\%$ of
the unit-cube diagonal), and the second held-out regime lies beyond the sampled
range entirely. And the training labels are $512$ coefficient draws crossed with
$64$ shared $(S,K,\tau)$ triples, none inside the region the decision occupies: the
trajectory starts $0.011$ from the nearest labelled point in range-normalised
coordinates and ends $0.169$ away (Figure~\ref{fig:coverage}a), so the decision is
evaluated under extrapolation in the contract coordinates as well as in the
coefficients. Residual collocation is redrawn over the full domain each epoch and is
exempt from the second condition. Two kinds of held-out cell follow: well-specified cells, where the generating
coefficients are the ones the surrogate is conditioned on and the test is
generalisation to excised coefficients, and misspecified cells, where the
trajectories come from perturbed or jump-augmented dynamics. At decision time every
arm receives the assumed-model coefficients of the held-out regime anchor, never
the perturbed coefficients generating the misspecified trajectories. Thus all arms
are wrong about the world in those cells and differ only in how well they represent
the assumed model.

The downstream decision is a discrete sequential control problem held fixed across
arms, so that derivative quality is the only varying factor. A controller holding
$\widehat{\partial_S u}$ units of the state rebalances at each of $43$ steps to a
horizon $T' = 0.17$ and settles against the true dynamics; the loss is the negative
of the resulting profit and the reported statistic is $\mathrm{CVaR}_{95}$
\citep{rockafellar2000optimization}. Every arm faces $10{,}000$ shared trajectories
per cell, drawn with the quadratic-exponential scheme of
\citet{andersen2008simple}, and pays proportional costs at tiers
$\{0, 1\%, 2\%\}$; the confirmatory cell is the combined coefficient perturbation at
$1\%$ over ten seeds. Three reference controllers run beside the arms. The first
uses the exact reference derivative; the second uses the variance-minimising combination
$\Delta_{\mathrm{MV}} = \partial_S u + (\rho\xi/S)\,\partial_v u$. Indeed,
$\operatorname{Cov}(du,dS)/\operatorname{Var}(dS)$ equals this expression under
the Heston diffusion associated with \eqref{eq:hestonpde}, making it the
local-risk-minimising ratio for this state in the sense of
\citet{follmer1991hedging}. The third is a
cost-aware policy suppressing small rebalances \citep{whalley1997asymptotic}. A
pre-registered falsifier separates a robustness reading of any gap from a cost
reading using excess turnover against the exact reference on identical trajectories
(Appendix~\ref{app:thresholds}).

The acceptance block, frozen before training and reproduced in
Appendix~\ref{app:thresholds}, requires a $\geq 10\%$ relative improvement with an
interval excluding zero at the confirmatory cell, the second-derivative rung to beat
the first there, demonstrated dose-response monotonicity, held-out second-derivative
and second-state RMSE each to fall by $\geq 15\%$ at value parity, and a $2\%$
negligibility floor on every contrast. Verdicts distinguish a fail from a \emph{null}, where the artifact was legitimately
absent, and an \emph{error}, where evaluation failed, since merging those would let
a broken run read as an absence of effect. Every contrast reports the registered
pooled bootstrap over shared trajectories with a per-seed companion.

The shared residual weight $\lambda_{\mathrm{pde}}$ was selected on validation only,
as the value minimising mean normalised validation RMSE over the solution and its
three derivatives for the residual-only arm, over the grid
$\{0, 10^{-2}, 10^{-1}, 1\}$, returning $10^{-2}$. Two properties of that
procedure govern Section~\ref{sec:readout}: the winner is the smallest non-zero
candidate, and the residual is normalised by a term whose scale inflates its
effective weight by roughly $79$ times, a design choice of ours rather than a
canonical one. Appendix~\ref{app:config} gives both, and the relative scale of a
residual term is known to govern what a physics-informed objective converges to
\citep{wang2021understanding}.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

Section~\ref{sec:delivers} reports what supervision does to held-out derivative
accuracy and Section~\ref{sec:reg} what it does to the registered downstream
endpoints. The three sections that follow account for the distance between those
two answers, through one decomposition: a controller's regret against the analytic
minimum-variance controller splits into approximation error, which
derivative supervision addresses, and control design, which it does not.

Registered intervals retain their pre-specified interpretation. Intervals and rank
comparisons introduced after the registered analysis are exploratory, descriptive,
and unadjusted for multiplicity.

\subsection{Held-out derivative accuracy}
\label{sec:delivers}

Held-out derivative accuracy is the clearest effect in the study and does not depend
on any choice examined later. Table~\ref{tab:greeks} reports held-out RMSE for all
three derivatives at both evaluation regimes, against two references: the
residual-only baseline the contract registers, and a value-only network carrying
neither residual nor derivative labels. Supervision cuts second-derivative and
second-state RMSE by $78$ to $96\%$ against either reference, attributable to the
second-derivative labels specifically, since that rung alone cuts held-out
second-derivative RMSE by $60\%$ and $59\%$, with value accuracy improving
alongside rather than being traded away.

Three controls establish that the gain is the curvature \emph{information} in the
labels rather than the smoothing effect of a curvature-shaped penalty
(Figure~\ref{fig:controls}). A label-free curvature $L^{2}$ penalty reaches held-out
second-derivative relative RMSE of $0.982$ against the unsupervised baseline's
$0.410$, worse than adding nothing; shuffled labels, preserving the label
distribution while destroying its correspondence to the state, reach $0.408$,
indistinguishable from adding nothing; and buying more value data instead of labels
saturates at $0.699$ held out, flat under a doubled-width retrain. Supervision
reaches $0.045$. The first two also degrade the first-derivative fit, so their
failure mode is training conflict rather than absence of signal, which is why they
evidence that correct curvature information is required and not a claim about
regularisers in general.
\subsection{Pre-registered downstream outcomes}
\label{sec:reg}

Table~\ref{tab:verdicts} reports every registered endpoint against its frozen
threshold. The confirmatory test fails: at the registered cell the relative
$\mathrm{CVaR}_{95}$ improvement of rung 3 over the residual-only baseline is $+0.02\%$
against a $10\%$ bar. Both the pooled path-level interval and its seed-level
companion cover zero, so neither analysis detects a difference. The order-attribution
endpoint, which carries the registered causal claim, also fails, and its failure is
not a null: the add-gamma rung is a reversal of $+0.0574$ in loss units, pooled
interval $[+0.0527, +0.0621]$ and per-seed interval $[+0.0311, +0.0856]$, both
excluding zero on the harmful side. Its relative size is $1.2\%$, below the
registered $2\%$ negligibility floor, so the reading is that gamma supervision is
statistically real, economically negligible, and harmful in sign at that cell,
rather than inert. The registered vocabulary, admitting only a pass and a
``failure equals honest null'', cannot express that outcome, which is recorded as a
defect of the vocabulary.

Two verdicts need their direction stated rather than their label repeated. The
accuracy endpoint fails on a conjoined value-parity guard and not on accuracy:
second-derivative and second-state reductions run from $0.864$ to $0.930$ against a

(Appendix~\ref{app:exhibits}).

\subsection{Sensitivity to the residual weight}
\label{sec:readout}

On the decision the result is best stated as a level rather than as a contrast.
Figure~\ref{fig:lambda} and Table~\ref{tab:lambda} report the two confirmatory arms
separately over a hundredfold range of the shared residual weight. The supervised
arm attains $\mathrm{CVaR}_{95}$ between $2.0758$ and $2.0905$ at every weight
including zero, against the exact-derivative reference's $2.0794$ on the same
trajectories. At every weight the zero-cost contrast against the residual-only arm
has a seed-level interval excluding zero across ten training runs, while that arm
moves from $2.1370$ to $3.0518$. The supervised arm's flatness is not an artefact of the residual being
negligible in the supervised objective: decomposing the final training loss, the
weighted residual is $53.3\%$ of the supervised arm's objective at the registered
weight, making it the largest term there, against $46.2\%$ for the three derivative
terms combined and $0.5\%$ for the value term. It is also substantial in the
residual-only objective at $42.8\%$, although its value term is larger. The residual
therefore carries material weight in both objectives but moves only the untreated
outcome. Supervision reaches
reference-level performance at held-out points and holds it against a
hyperparameter with no principled setting, one that moves the untreated arm by more
than the treatment is worth.

Read as a difference instead of a level, the same sweep behaves quite differently,
and the difference is what the contract registers. The contrast is monotone in
$\lambda_{\mathrm{pde}}$ and convex in its logarithm, spanning an order of magnitude
with no interior optimum, and the registered weight sits where the curve is
steepest. Since the supervised arm does not move, the contrast measures the
untreated arm's handicap: $93.6\%$ of the registered contrast is repair of damage
the residual does to that arm. At $\lambda_{\mathrm{pde}} = 0$ the two arms are
bitwise identical to the registered value-only and residual-free arms, so the sweep
also completes the residual-by-supervision factorial at ten seeds, giving an
interaction of $15.7$ times. Against the undamaged value-only control, the only
reference the residual has not degraded, supervision is worth $0.0611$ in loss units
at zero cost, $2.9\%$ relative, with pooled interval $[-0.0672, -0.0588]$ and a
seed-level interval excluding zero across ten training runs. This is above the
pre-registered $2\%$ floor and an order of magnitude below the contrast at the
registered weight.

Two selection failures produced that weight, and only the first is repaired by a
finer grid: the registered value is the worst of the six points under the contract's
own criterion, and, more seriously, on the selection region the residual helps the
untreated arm while at the states the decision visits it hurts, so the criterion
improves in the direction that degrades the decision. Since the evaluation regimes
are excised from validation, no validation point can report that. The registered
cost tier is separately a design error: its $1\%$ per-side rate is $100$ times the
$0.01\%$ ETF spread reported by \citet{armstrong2024deep}, or $200$ times the
corresponding half-spread. It licenses no rescue, so any cost-tier reframing is
withdrawn rather than relabelled
(Appendix~\ref{app:exhibits}).

What the residual does is measurable on the derivative field itself, and it is the
mechanism of the damage rather than a benefit of supervision. On the decision
trajectories the untreated arm's first derivative is a flattened sigmoid, regression
slope $0.637$ against the reference: a slope deficit, not a level bias, and visible
directly in Figure~\ref{fig:problem}b. It therefore under-trades the reference by
$38\%$ while supervision restores reference-level turnover, which runs against the
registered cost channel, whose falsifier presumed an over-trading baseline. The
registered robustness channel fares no better, since $86\%$ of the zero-cost
contrast is already present under correct dynamics. The account consistent with
every cell is approximation quality of the first derivative feeding discrete-time
tracking error, which \citet{noguer2026derivative} bounds in theory and for which
the registered two-by-two has no bin.

\subsection{Where the derivative error is measured}
\label{sec:region}

The registered accuracy metric is scored on the full grid ($S \in [50,150]$,
$K \in [60,140]$, $\tau \in [0.04,1]$) while the decision runs at one contract
coordinate over $\tau \in [0.08, 0.25]$. Restricting the identical metric to the
region the decision occupies (Figure~\ref{fig:coverage}b) reverses the residual-only
arm against the value-only network at both held-out regimes, on both derivatives,
in all five seeds; the residual-only arm is the only one that degrades on entering
that region. The residual's dominant term $\tfrac12 vS^{2}\partial_{SS}u$ is
stiffest near the non-smooth initial condition and its root mean square is $1.45$
times larger inside, consistent with the reversal without establishing it. The
consequence is method-level: two models can rank one way under the protocol the
literature reports and the opposite way under the accuracy the decision consumes.
The two axes do not conflict when measured in one region, where first-derivative
RMSE orders the arms exactly as zero-cost $\mathrm{CVaR}_{95}$ does.

A ranking reversal is an existence result, and on its own it does not license the
obvious recommendation. Tested directly, restricting the metric to the decision's
region is barely better than the grid at predicting realised cost: pooled pairwise
concordance over seven arms, five seeds and two held-out regimes is $0.733$
restricted against $0.710$ on the grid, and for the first derivative at the
extrapolated regime the restricted metric is worse. These pooled values aggregate
all $840$ arm-pair comparisons over four derivatives, including vanna; over the
three derivatives displayed in Table~\ref{tab:metric}, the corresponding values are
$0.716$ and $0.687$. The region is the wrong object.
What it crudely approximates is the error weighted by the occupancy measure the
decision induces, $\mathbb{E}_{\mu^{\pi}}[(\hat{D}u - Du)^{2}]$, evaluated at the
states at which the controller actually forms positions. Evaluated on $1{,}000$
deterministically regenerated seed-keyed trajectories per seed, that quantity predicts
realised cost better than either alternative on every derivative and on both
statistics (Table~\ref{tab:metric}). The recommendation is therefore the estimand,
with the region-restricted metric named as its crude proxy rather than as the fix.
Four caveats travel with it. The occupancy metric differs from the other two in
weighting \emph{and} in parameter cell, so only the grid-versus-region comparison is
clean; it is one cell over five seeds, which makes it suggestive rather than settled;
reference derivatives at its off-grid path states come directly from the
characteristic-function evaluator used by the decision engine rather than the
masked four-leg consensus; and label support inside that region is itself thin,
masked at $1.30$ times the outside rate
(Appendix~\ref{app:exhibits}).

\subsection{Reconstruction from the supervised fields}
\label{sec:mvbuild}

The exact-derivative reference used by the registered scorecard is not the
analytic minimum-variance controller.
Driving the same trajectories with $\Delta_{\mathrm{MV}}$ gives misspecified
zero-cost $\mathrm{CVaR}_{95}$ of $1.670$ against the exact-derivative reference's
$2.082$, $19.8\%$ better, and $10.6\%$ better under correct dynamics. Decomposed,
the supervised arm's regret against that controller is $0.4261$ in loss units, of
which $0.0141$ is approximation error against the exact derivative and the rest is
the design of the exact-derivative policy itself: $96.7\%$ of what is left to win is
control design, a ratio of $29.2$ to one. For the untreated arm the split inverts to
$70.8\%$ approximation, which is what being the damaged arm looks like. At the
accuracy the supervised arm reaches, the registered exact-derivative controller
therefore leaves little approximation headroom; most remaining loss comes from the
choice of controller. The same direction-matters account explains why a
systematically biased second-derivative label produces the best learned controller
within the registered delta-only policy class at zero cost: its bias lies in the
variance-minimising direction (Appendix~\ref{app:exhibits}).

Because $\Delta_{\mathrm{MV}}$ is assembled from $\partial_S u$ and $\partial_v u$,
any arm carrying both can build it from its own outputs with no further reference.
This is where the accuracy result and the decision result meet. It was run after the
registered analysis was complete and is exploratory, moving no registered verdict,
but it reuses the frozen checkpoints, the same shared trajectories every other arm
faces, and the same paired bootstrap the contract registers. Every arm supervising
the first two derivatives reconstructs the variance-minimising controller to within
the pre-registered $2\%$ floor (Table~\ref{tab:mv}): the third rung lands at
$1.6701$ against the analytic $1.6696$, a pooled difference whose interval covers
zero. Rung 2 is lower still at $1.6563$, $0.80\%$ better than the analytic
construction with a pooled interval excluding zero on the favourable side. Because
that difference remains inside the $2\%$ floor, it is evidence of favourable bias
in the direction the decision rewards, consistent with the biased-label result,
not an unqualified policy improvement. The arms without second-derivative labels
do not reconstruct within the floor, leaving $3.27\%$ and $3.40\%$; their
seed-level intervals exclude zero across five training runs, while the
residual-damaged arm leaves $42.9\%$. Second-derivative supervision is therefore sufficient for the
reconstruction without second-state supervision, though necessity is not
established, since the ladder never trained a first-plus-second-state arm. The
mechanism is in Table~\ref{tab:greeks}: supervising the second derivative also
improves the \emph{unsupervised} second-state derivative that $\Delta_{\mathrm{MV}}$
consumes. Derivative accuracy does transfer to a better decision, once the decision
consumes the fields supervision improves.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Every registered efficacy endpoint of this study failed. The large contrast at the
registered residual weight is not a stable treatment effect: it is a monotone
readout of that weight, most of it repair of damage the residual does to the
untreated arm in exactly the region the decision occupies. Against the undamaged
value-only control, supervision produces a real but small $2.9\%$ zero-cost gain,
well below the registered $10\%$ bar. The design instead establishes where this
scorecard sends the credit wrong. Accuracy is scored on a grid while the decision
consumes it on a trajectory; in an exploratory one-cell comparison, error weighted
by the decision's occupancy measure predicts the outcome better than the grid or a
hand-drawn region. Direction matters as much as size, which is why a biased label
produces the best learned controller within the registered delta-only policy class.
Finally, $96.7\%$ of the gap separating the supervised arm from the analytic
minimum-variance controller is control design, leaving little approximation
headroom at the accuracy supervision reaches.

Supervision itself does deliver: held-out derivative accuracy that three
information-free controls cannot reproduce, reference-level decision cost held
across a hundredfold range of a training weight that is simultaneously the largest
term in its own objective, and enough fidelity in two first derivatives to
reconstruct a controller that beats the exact-derivative reference.

We would ask five things of any study reporting derivative accuracy for a surrogate
whose derivatives are spent downstream. Prefer error under the occupancy measure
the decision induces to error on a generic parameter grid, while treating our
one-cell evidence as suggestive rather than settled. A hand-drawn region is only a
crude proxy and bought little here.
Report arms as levels and not only as contrasts, because a contrast moves when
either arm moves and ours moved elevenfold while the treated arm sat still. Resolve
hyperparameter grids finely enough to locate their own optimum. Check that the
reference the decision is scored against is the best one evaluated for the relevant
cost regime, since ours was not. And fix a negligibility floor in advance, because pooled resolution over
$10^{4}$ trajectories finds statistically real effects that are worth nothing.

Five limitations bound these claims. The magnitude of the residual damage depends on
our own normalisation of that term, which inflates its effective weight by roughly
$79$ times, so the size of the pathology should not be read as generic; what
generalises in form is the pair of selection failures. The controls, the
reconstruction result and the residual-free arm carry five seeds rather than ten,
and the occupancy comparison is one parameter cell. The decision terminates at
$\tau = 0.08$, so the null is scoped to that horizon rather than showing the design
blind to curvature effects, which also act earlier. Label support inside the
decision's region is thin, and the accuracy claim there is scored on a mask that
removes $1.30$ times as many points as outside it. And labels and reference come
from the same oracle family on a small smooth network over a two-state parabolic
problem, so this is generalisation of a solution field rather than discovery of an
unknown one, and not a claim about surrogates in general or validation of a
deployable trading or risk-management system. The natural next
experiments are the missing first-plus-second-state arm, supervising toward
$\Delta_{\mathrm{MV}}$ directly, and dosing label bias rather than label variance.

\section{Full configuration}
\label{app:config}

\paragraph{Residual-weight selection.} The shared weight was selected as the value
minimising mean normalised validation RMSE over the solution and its
three derivatives for the residual-only arm over the grid
$\{0, 10^{-2}, 10^{-1}, 1\}$, returning $10^{-2}$. Two properties of that procedure
matter for Section~\ref{sec:readout}. The winner is the smallest non-zero candidate
and beats $\lambda_{\mathrm{pde}} = 0$ by only $3.4\%$, so the grid selected at its
own boundary and $(0, 10^{-2})$ was never explored. And the residual is normalised
by $\mathrm{mean}[(ru^{\star})^{2}]$, the squared discount term evaluated on the
labels: model-free, but our design choice rather than a canonical one. Its root mean
square is $0.527$ while the residual's own dominant term is $4.674$, so a nominal
weight carries roughly $79$ times the weight a scale-matched normalisation would
give it, and the relative scale of a residual term is known to govern what a
physics-informed objective converges to \citep{wang2021understanding}. The contract
further pre-registered a robustness row re-running the confirmatory contrast with
the weight sourced from the treated arm instead; that value is $0$, and the row is
reported rather than deferred.

\paragraph{Excess-turnover convention.} The registered mechanism statistic is the
total traded position of an arm in excess of the exact-derivative reference on the
same trajectories,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tex}
T_{\mathrm{ex}}
=\sum_{i=0}^{43}\left|\widehat{\Delta}_{i}-\widehat{\Delta}_{i-1}\right|
-\sum_{i=0}^{43}\left|\Delta^{\circ}_{i}-\Delta^{\circ}_{i-1}\right|,
\qquad
\widehat{\Delta}_{-1}=\widehat{\Delta}_{43}
=\Delta^{\circ}_{-1}=\Delta^{\circ}_{43}=0.
\end{equation}
The $i=0$ terms charge initial acquisition, the $i=43$ terms charge final
liquidation, and the intervening terms charge changes in position. Thus
$T_{\mathrm{ex}}<0$ denotes an arm that trades less than the reference.

\section{Cost profile, dose-response, and supporting exhibits}
\label{app:exhibits}

\paragraph{What the mask removes, and where.} The oracle mask drops $9.79\%$ of
label cells. It does not select the hard points: masked cells have lower mean
curvature than retained ones ($0.0042$ against $0.0091$) and sit at short maturity
and deep out-of-the-money, where a \emph{relative} agreement tolerance is hardest to
satisfy, so the mask is a tolerance artefact rather than difficulty selection.
It is not neutral in location, however. Inside the region the decision occupies the
mask rate is $0.1233$ against $0.0948$ outside, a ratio of $1.30$, and two maturity
nodes the trajectory passes through are almost entirely removed, $98.8\%$ at
$\tau = 0.093$ and $84.8\%$ at $\tau = 0.115$. The in-region accuracy claim of
Section~\ref{sec:region} is therefore scored where label support is thinnest, which
is stated with the claim rather than in a footnote.

\paragraph{What derivative labels cost.} Derivative labels are near-free from the
characteristic-function leg and ruinous from the grid solver, which matters for
anyone deciding whether to buy them. Measured on one machine over $64$ contract
points, single process, so ratios transfer and seconds do not: analytic
differentiation through the characteristic-function integrand costs $1.0$ times a
price call, since one quadrature serves the value and every derivative;
finite-difference stencils on the transform prices cost $8.4$ times; the
Monte-Carlo leg $1.9$ times at a tenth of production paths, so roughly $19$ times at
production; and the Craig--Sneyd solve $688$ times. Read against the
information-matching plateau, which puts derivative labels at roughly three times
their value-point budget in finite-difference-equivalent units, labels pay for
themselves from the cheap leg by a wide margin, and the expensive leg is required
only for the Feller-stressed band.

\section{Oracle validation, mask-neutrality panels, and the headroom ladder}
\label{app:oracle}

\paragraph{Construction.} The primary leg differentiates the trap-free
characteristic-function branch of \citet{albrecher2007little}, with Fourier pricing per \citet{carrmadan1999option} and the cosine expansion of
\citet{fang2008novel}, before discretisation rather than after; the second
takes high-order finite differences on the same price grid, the third is Monte-Carlo
pathwise, and the fourth a Craig--Sneyd ADI solver \citep{craig1988alternating} routed to points
where the variance process is stressed. The first two share the characteristic-function
machinery, so the four legs are three independent witnesses at most.

\paragraph{Fourth-leg diagnosis.} On \texttt{feller\_violating\_volvol}, the ADI
leg's vega disagreed with the characteristic-function leg systematically, exceeding
it on $87.9\%$ of grid points, growing monotonically in maturity from $0.0$ at
$\tau = 0.04$ to $0.0087$ at $\tau = 1.0$, and masking $59.4\%$ of that regime's
vega grid. Four candidate causes were eliminated in order
(Table~\ref{tab:adi}). The survivor was variance-domain truncation: at the previous
default the variance domain is cut inside the mass of the $\xi = 0.60$ regime and
the artificial boundary contaminates $v$-direction derivatives. Raising $v_{\max}$
from $1.6$ to $3.2$ changes the median disagreement from $4.0\mathrm{e}{-}05$ to
$2.8\mathrm{e}{-}05$, so $1.6$ is converged, and \texttt{near\_feller} is flat under
the same sweep, which localises the effect to broad-$\xi$ regimes. The worst
relative error on the affected regime improved from $3.56\mathrm{e}{-}03$ to
$1.57\mathrm{e}{-}03$ against a $5\mathrm{e}{-}03$ bar. What adjudicated the
disagreement was narrower than four legs suggests: the Monte-Carlo band exceeded the
disagreement on $85\%$ of points and could not break the tie. On that regime the
vega uncertainty is $1.33$, dominated by Monte-Carlo noise at $\xi = 0.60$ and
binding on $70\%$ of points, which no solver change lowers.

\paragraph{Mask-neutrality panels.} The Feller-band panel shows the affected band
halving from $0.5192$ to $0.2434$ while every other band is bit-identical, the
control confirming that the change touched only the fourth leg. That band remains
elevated against roughly $0.09$ elsewhere, its excess falling from $5.8\times$ to
$2.7\times$. The marginal-distribution panel moved in both directions: $\kappa$,
$\theta$, and $\xi$ improved ($0.0645 \to 0.0287$, $0.1787 \to 0.1201$,
$0.1284 \to 0.0383$), while $\rho$ and $v_0$ worsened ($0.1242 \to 0.1492$,
$0.0832 \to 0.1266$). The improvement in $\theta$ and $\xi$ is mechanical, since the
Feller ratio is $2\kappa\theta/\xi^{2}$ and band-concentrated masking is itself a
$\theta$ and $\xi$ shift; the deterioration in $\rho$ and $v_0$ follows from the
masked set shrinking by roughly $12\%$ and changing composition. The fix is
therefore not a uniform improvement and is not presented as one. The
curvature-decile panel improved at every decile including the highest
($0.1221 \to 0.1010$), which is the one that matters for gamma supervision, and the
contract's stated failure mode for that panel, the mask rate rising with curvature,
does not occur.

\paragraph{Headroom-gate ladder.} Rungs at
$\sigma_{\mathrm{rel}} \in \{0.05, 0.10, 0.15\}$ are decision rungs;
$\{0.20, 0.40\}$ are diagnostic, swept and plotted identically but excluded from the
decision scan (Table~\ref{tab:ladder}). The bound
$\texttt{clipped\_frac\_max} = 0.25$ was not raised to re-admit $0.20$, whose
per-seed maximum straddles it, since a rung whose eligibility depends on which seeds
were drawn is not a decision rung. The bound is structural rather than statistical:
above it the corrupted arm is a saturated hedger pinned at the delta bound over much
of the state space, whose gamma error is the oracle's own $-\Gamma$ rather than the
calibrated field, and no trained network's Greek error behaves that way. Both
measured pilot points lie inside the region of validity, at clipped fractions of
$0.063$ and $0.000$.
